### [VOLUME 1: THE MUD, THE BEAST & THE D-TIER BLOOD-PIT]
#### CHAPTER 3: THE SAND, THE STEEL, AND THE FIRST SPARK

The registration clerk’s fingernails were black with dried ink and animal fat.

He sat behind a grease-stained oak trestle table placed under a sagging canvas awning, a copper scale and an open, dog-eared parchment ledger before him. Behind his stool stood a tall, iron-bound weapons rack where dozens of pitted, notched blades hung from rusted leather loops like dead fish in a market stall.

"Name?" the clerk grunted, not bothering to lift his bloodshot eyes from the parchment.

"Neel."

"House, faction, or patron guild?"

"None."

The clerk dipped a split-reed quill into a clay pot of black ink, scratching a jagged mark into the margin. "Unindexed stray. Permanent residence?"

"Transitory," Neel answered smoothly. In suburban Mumbai, when a municipal officer asked where you lived and you didn't want him showing up at your door, you never said *nowhere*—you said *in transit*. It sounded professional and gave them nothing to seize.

The quill paused. The clerk finally glanced up, squinting through heavy, alcohol-bloated lids at Neel’s soaked denim jacket, the torn canvas sneakers, and the low-slung, dark-gray hound sitting solidly against his shins.

"That mutt from Vane’s scrap-pen?" the clerk asked, an ugly smirk twitching through his graying stubble. "The one with half an ear chewed off by a Calderan pit-drake? What's its registry mark?"

"Register him as Kuro," Neel answered, giving the official ledger a crisp Common Concord label, while his hand scratched behind the dog's torn ear. *"Chal re Sheru,"* he whispered under his breath. "And his contract is transferred to my purse."

The hound let out a faint, affirmative huff through its black nostrils, leaning its lean shoulder harder against Neel’s knee. 

The clerk snorted, shaking his head. "Kuro. Right. D-Tier Rookie Feeder Circuit, bout four. Rules are simple enough even an unindexed whelp can memorize them: weapons are blunted on the edge, live on the point. You yield by dropping your steel and kneeling with both palms flat in the sand. If you kill an opponent after the referee strikes the gong, the city watch hangs you from the South Gate arch. If your beast tears a throat after the bell, we roast it on a spit. Understand?"

"Clear as glass," Neel said.

"Pick your iron from the third tier on the rack. The upper hooks are reserved for fighters with guild chits."

Neel walked over to the rack. 

The third tier was where the refuse lived. Bent iron daggers with loose hemp-wrapped hilts, wood-hafted hand axes with splintered handles, and dented bronze bucklers that looked like they had stopped runaway cart wheels. 

Neel didn't reach for the biggest blade. 

Growing up watching street brawls outside suburban railway stations had taught him that the idiot who picks the heaviest crowbar always swings wide, exhausts his forearms in twenty seconds, and gets stabbed by the guy holding a six-inch screwdriver.

He picked a short, double-edged iron gladius—twenty inches of dull gray steel with a forward-weighted leaf blade. The edges were nicked, but the point had been reground to a wicked, triangular needle-taper. The hilt was raw ash-wood wrapped in tarred cord, fitting his grip with a tight, abrasive bite. Next to it, he grabbed a rectangular boiled-leather arm-guard, strapping it securely to his left forearm.

He turned the blade in his hand. It weighed roughly two pounds. Balanced two inches in front of the crossguard. 

*Functional.*

"Bout four! Red sand gate!" an arena marshal shouted from the end of a dark stone tunnel leading beneath the spectator bleachers. "Unindexed rookie Neel and scrapper versus House Orlov recruit Boras and Sand-Boar!"

Neel adjusted his backpack straps, leaving the bag against the arena registry bench under the clerk’s disinterested eye. He patted his right jacket pocket, feeling the solid rectangular weight of his dead smartphone against his ribs. 

"Stay close, Sheru," Neel murmured, stepping toward the dark tunnel.

The hound trotted alongside him, its yellow eyes locked forward, its claws clicking sharply against the wet flagstones.

The tunnel smelled of damp sulfur, horse liniment, and the cold iron tang of spilled blood. At the far end, daylight glared through an iron portcullis. 

Beyond the bars lay the sand-pit.

It wasn't the magnificent, hundred-thousand-seat coliseum described in the city annals. This was the bottom gutter: an eighty-foot oval carved out of the exterior mud, bordered by twelve-foot wooden palisades reinforced with iron banding. Above the palisade, roughly three hundred spectators—merchants in greasy wool coats, off-duty caravan drivers, mercenary scouts, and slum betting-agents—leaned over timber railings, shouting bets and throwing peanut husks into the pit.

The iron portcullis ground upward with a screech of rusted chains.

Neel stepped out onto the red-tinted sand. 

The sand was damp, heavy, and churned into deep troughs from three previous bouts. Across the oval, emerging from the opposing tunnel, came his opponent.

The man called Boras was at least twenty-five, with a barrel chest covered in a stiff, grease-cured leather cuirass bearing a faded green boar crest. In his right hand, he carried a three-foot iron-headed mace; on his left arm sat an iron-rimmed round shield. 

Waddling beside him on thick, stubby legs was a four-hundred-pound beast that looked like a cross between a razorback pig and an armadillo: a Stage-1 Sand-Boar covered in thick, bony osteoderm plates, its snout armed with two upturned, yellowed ivory tusks.

"Five to one on the boar!" a voice yelled from the bleachers. "Ten coppers the mutt runs before the first charge!"

Boras grinned, spitting a wad of phlegm into the sand. "Look at this garbage. The guild master gave me an alley stray in cotton trousers. Hey, kid—drop the toothpick and crawl back into the sewer. I won't even have to clean my mace."

Neel didn't answer. 

His 200-IQ reading-room radar was already humming, analyzing every detail of the man's stance with cold, computational clarity.

*Right foot turned thirty degrees outward. He’s putting all his weight on the front heel. His cuirass is too tight across the shoulder blades—every time he lifts that mace, the leather binds his deltoid, slowing his recovery by half a second. And the boar...*

Neel glanced at the beast. Its breathing was shallow, its piggish red eyes locked entirely on the hound. It had been trained on a single command track: charge in a straight line, gore with the left tusk, pin against the wall.

*Predictable.*

High above the center of the palisade, a stone platform jutted out. An arena referee in a black-and-gold surcoat struck a massive bronze gong with a padded mallet.

*BUMMMMMMM.*

The deep, resonant note echoed off the granite city walls, vibrating through the sand under Neel’s sneakers.

"Tear him, Tusker!" Boras roared, slamming his mace against his shield with a metallic crash.

The Sand-Boar squealed—a high-pitched, deafening shriek—and lowered its head. Its thick hooves churned the red sand into flying plumes as it launched forward in an explosive, direct charge, hurtling straight at Neel like a boulder kicked off a cliff.

"Sheru—wait!" Neel barked softly.

The hound tensed, its hackles raised, its muscles coiled like steel wire, but it stayed rigidly glued to Neel’s left heel. 

Thirty yards. Twenty yards. 

The ground trembled under the boar’s four-hundred-pound bulk. The tusks were pointed directly at Neel’s groin.

Ten yards. 

Five yards.

"NOW! RIGHT!"

Neel didn't dive backward like an amateur. He exploded laterally to his right, driving off his back foot in a sharp, explosive sidestep he had practiced dodging Mumbai motorcyclists on wet pedestrian crossings. 

The boar blew past his left hip by six inches, its massive head unable to correct its momentum on the slick red sand. 

*Crack!*

Sheru lunged. 

The hound didn't attack the armored skull or the bony back plates. With terrifying combat instinct, it slipped under the boar’s flank and clamped its needle-sharp jaws directly onto the beast's rear left hock—the unarmored tendon connecting the hoof to the joint. 

The boar shrieked in agony, its rear leg giving out as Sheru's weight twisted its balance. The four-hundred-pound creature violently careened sideways, crashing into the wet sand in a sprawling cloud of dust.

"You mangy cur!" Boras bellowed, charging forward with his heavy mace raised high, his face purple with rage.

He swung. 

The three-foot iron mace cut through the air with a vicious, whistling hum—aimed straight at Neel’s skull.

Neel didn't try to block a two-pound iron head with a leather arm-guard. He stepped *inside* the swing. 

As the mace whistled over his ducked head, Neel drove his left shoulder into Boras’s chest, using the man’s own forward momentum to jam his weapon arm. The leather binding across Boras’s back did exactly what Neel had predicted: it caught, locking the man's arm behind him for an agonizing fraction of a second.

Neel slammed the pommel of his gladius into the side of Boras’s knee. 

Bone popped with a sickening crunch. 

Boras screamed, his leg collapsing under him as he fell onto his back in the sand, dropping his heavy shield. But the man was a seasoned pit fighter; through the blinding pain, he whipped his left fist around, his iron-plated knuckle catching Neel across the jaw.

The world went white for half a second. 

Neel stumbled back, his mouth filling with blood, his boots sliding through the sand. 

Across the pit, the Sand-Boar had scrambled back to its three good legs, shaking off the hound with a violent snap of its head that sent Sheru skidding across the sand, leaving a gouge of red earth. The boar turned, lowering its bloodied tusks for a final, lethal lunge straight at Neel’s unprotected chest.

And Boras was scrambling to his feet, raising the iron mace with both hands.

*Two on one.*

*No blue menus. No level-up. No divine avatar descending from the sky.*

In that split second of mortal terror, Neel’s heart hammered against his ribs like a sledgehammer. 

*Thump-thump. Thump-thump. THUMP-THUMP.*

Across the pit, twenty paces away, Sheru pushed itself up on trembling legs, let out a ragged, defiant snarl, and locked eyes with Neel.

The connection snapped shut.

Through the cold arena air, Neel felt it: a sudden, terrifying surge of heat rushing from his chest straight down both arms, vibrating through his 72,000 newly unsealed Nadis like lightning coursing through dry copper wire. 

His pulse and Sheru’s pulse merged into one deafening, synchronized rhythm inside his eardrums.

*One-two. One-two.*

Time seemed to drag, stretching out like pulled molten glass. 

Neel didn't think. He didn't recite a formula. His 21st-century secular skepticism vanished beneath raw, primal street survival.

He dropped the gladius from his right hand into the sand. 

He lunged forward, ducking under Boras’s downward mace strike, closing the distance until his chest was an inch from the man’s leather cuirass. 

Neel shoved his bare right palm directly onto Boras’s exposed, sweating sternum—right over the solar plexus.

"What—?" Boras gasped.

Neel clenched his fingers, willing every drop of the burning static in his veins into his palm.

*DISCHARGE.*

**CRACK-ZZZZZZZZT!**

A blinding, high-frequency arc of violet-blue electricity exploded from Neel’s fingertips. 

The concussive shockwave ripped through the leather cuirass, through Boras’s ribcage, and directly into the man’s central nervous system. 

Boras’s eyes rolled back until only the whites showed. His entire muscular system seized in an instantaneous, violent convulsion; his back arched backward with a sickening snap, the iron mace flew ten feet into the air, and he dropped face-first into the red sand like a felled ox, completely limp and unconscious.

Across the pit, the Sand-Boar skidded to a dead halt, its tiny eyes widening in primal horror at the smell of scorched ozone and fried leather. It looked at its unconscious master, looked at the boy whose right hand was still venting thin curls of blue smoke, squealed once, turned around, and bolted straight back into its gate tunnel.

Silence fell over the pit.

The three hundred spectators along the palisade railings froze, their shouts dying in their throats. 

Even the arena referee stood paralyzed on his stone platform, his mallet hovering inches from the gong, staring down at the unindexed kid in the wet denim jacket standing over House Orlov’s recruit.

Neel stood panting, his chest heaving, his right palm throbbing with an intense, numb ache. 

Tiny sparks still danced harmlessly across his fingernails before fading into the damp air.

*Holy shit,* his brain whispered. *That wasn't static electricity.*

He turned his head slowly. Sheru limped over, its tail giving two tentative, low wags, and pressed its warm, scarred head against Neel’s left knee. 

Neel bent down, picked up his blunted iron gladius, and wiped the blood from his split lip with the back of his sleeve.

High above, the referee finally swallowed hard, brought his mallet down, and struck the bronze gong.

*BUMMMMMMM.*

"Victor!" the referee’s voice boomed across the silent arena, tinged with disbelief. "Bout four victor: the unindexed rookie, Neel! Claim your purse at the gate!"

A sudden, raucous roar exploded from the bleachers—some cheering the massive payout on a five-to-one underdog, others cursing Boras for losing their copper, all of them shouting and pointing at the blue-sparking rookie.

Ten minutes later, Neel sat on the stone steps of the paymaster’s hut outside the trench.

On the step beside him lay:
- Ten triangular copper Panas, heavy and clinking with real purchasing power.
- A notched wooden token stamped with the South Gate seal in fresh red wax: *Official Central City Transit Pass*.
- A thick, warm loaf of crusty barley bread that smelled like heaven.
- A small earthenware jar of green healing tallow.

Neel took five of the copper Panas, walked over to where Vane and the mallet-wielder were standing in stunned silence, and tossed the coins onto their timber bench. 

"Five Panas," Neel said, his voice flat, steady, and chillingly calm. "Paid in full. The hound stays with me."

Neither man said a single word. They simply nodded, staring at his right hand as if it held a primed explosive.

Neel walked back to the steps, sat down next to Sheru, and unscrewed the jar of tallow. He gently worked the soothing green paste into the gash along the dog's ribs and over the raw stump of its torn ear. The hound whined softly once, then leaned into his touch, resting its chin on his knee.

Neel broke the warm barley bread in half. 

He didn't take the bigger half. He broke it clean down the middle, set half in front of the hound’s snout, and shoved the other half into his own mouth. 

The bread was coarse, heavy, and tasted of stone-ground grain and salt. It was the best thing he had ever eaten in nineteen years of life.

He chewed slowly, watching the massive South Gate of Central City swing wide open under the afternoon light, its granite arches soaring into a sky filled with hanging, fractured worlds.

He pulled his cracked, dead smartphone from his pocket, touched the fractured glass one last time, and slipped it back deep against his heart.

"All right, Sheru," Neel said, swallowing the last crust and standing up with his short iron blade sheathed at his hip. "Let's go see what this city costs."
