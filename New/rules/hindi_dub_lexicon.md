## 0. The One Law

**Hindi is written first. English follows.**

The Hindi file is not a translation of the English file. It is the primary authored text for the dub track. The English file is the second draft, derived from the Hindi scene, not the other way round.

If a Hindi line reads like English grammar wearing Hindi words, it is wrong — even if every word is correct.

## 4. English That Must Stay English (Roman, inside Hindi prose)

Never transliterate these into Devanagari. A Hindi reader of anime dubs expects them in Roman.

- **Places:** Central City, South Flats, Staging Ground, South Trench, Feeder Slums, Feeder Sand-Pits, Five-Arch Rotunda, Central Shard, Caldera
- **Classes & ranks:** Drifter, unindexed, D-Rank, C-Rank, Stage-1, Dusk-Scrapper
- **Currency:** Panas, Masha, Aether-Rupee
- **Beasts & items:** Lava-Bull, Beast-Cart, Runic
- **Names:** Neel, Sheru, Kuro — and the ledger name `Kuro` only appears from Chapter 3 onward
- **Tech that Neel brought with him:** smartphone, backpack, denim, sneakers, battery, screen

Rule of thumb: **the world's institutions stay Roman. Neel's body and feelings stay Hindi.**

---

## 5. Banned English Litter

Do not sprinkle English filler into Hindi narration for flavour. It reads like a bad fan translation.

Banned: `literally`, `basically`, `actually`, `obviously`, `kind of`, `sort of`, `vibe`, `scene` (as in "ye kya scene hai" — allowed only in dialogue, never narration), `hound pup`, `cross-cut scar`, `bio-rhythm`, `floating box`, `rendering glitch`.

If the concept is technical, either commit to the Roman English term (Section 4) or say it plainly in Hindi. Never half-and-half.

---

## 6. Rhythm Rules

1. **One idea per sentence.** Hindi dub narration breathes in short clauses. If a sentence needs two commas and a dash, split it.
2. **Dashes are rare.** Max one `—` per paragraph. English uses them for rhythm; Hindi uses `।` and short sentences.
3. **No exclamation marks in narration.** Only in dialogue, and sparingly.
4. **`।` ends every narration and dialogue line.** No `.` unless the line ends in a Roman term inside a quotation.
5. **Monologue format:** Internal thoughts are written in standard clean italics (`*...*`) without any tag labels.
6. **Read-aloud test:** if a line cannot be spoken in one breath by a dubbing artist, it is too long.

---

## 7. Person & Tense Lock

The dub track runs in **third person, past tense** — the standard anime-dub narrator voice.

- ✅ `Neel ने सिर उठाया।`
- ❌ `मैंने सिर उठाया।`

**Exception — one paragraph maximum:** a chapter may open with a single first-person hook line in the narrator's own voice, immediately handing over to third person. `20_Season_1_Chapter_2_Hindi.md` does this (`इज़्ज़त-विज़्ज़ता सब बाद की बात थी, भाई।`). More than one paragraph and the chapter is broken.

Inner monologue stays first person rendered in clean italics.

## 8. Pre-Publish QA (run on every Hindi file)

1. **Zero literary hits:** search `क्षमता, निर्णय, परिस्थिति, किंतु, अतः, तथापि, प्रारंभ, क्षण, नेत्र, वदन, हृदय, जल, वायु, अग्नि`. All must be 0.
2. **Zero Devanagari place names:** search `साउथ, स्टेजिंग, ट्रेंच, ड्रिफ्टर, अनइंडेक्स्ड, पाना`. All must be 0.
3. **`।` count ≥ sentence count.** No stray English full stops in narration.
4. **Dash count:** `—` should be under one per paragraph on average.
5. **Read-aloud:** speak the first 200 words. If you run out of breath or hit a clause you would never say, rewrite it.
6. **Person check:** count `मैं` outside italic monologue blocks. It must stay inside the single opening paragraph, if used at all.
