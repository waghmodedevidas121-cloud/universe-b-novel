# SEASON 1 • VOLUME 1: THE MUD, THE BEAST & THE D-TIER BLOOD-PIT

## PROLOGUE: THE GLITCH IN THE RAIN

The rank stench of rotting garbage, burnt diesel exhaust, and overflowing open gutters hit the back of his throat with suffocating force.

Outside Dadar station, the torrential monsoon had raged unbroken for six hours. Two feet of murky, stagnant water flooded the streets, carrying an oily slurry of vehicle grease, plastic refuse, and decaying vegetables. Exhaust pipes were submerged, and the frantic, dissonant blare of truck air-horns slammed against eardrums like iron pipes.

Leaning casually against the rusted pedestrian railing beneath the overpass stood Neel.

Slung over his shoulder was a waterproof leather pack protecting a delicate payload of high-density silicon wafers and micro-controllers—special-ordered from Germany. Personally navigating the crowded alleys of Lamington Road to haul prototype hardware was undoubtedly the dumbest decision he’d made all week, especially since his custom sports coupe sat hydro-locked in three feet of floodwater half a kilometer behind him.

A crimson municipal BEST bus plowed through a massive crater in the road at full speed.

*SPLASH!*

A heavy sheet of black gutter sludge surged into the air.

Clearing the railing, the foul spray slammed squarely across Neel’s face, neck, and bespoke ash-grey cashmere hoodie.

Cold, greasy runoff trickled down his jawline.

Neel stood motionless for five full seconds. He didn't flinch. He didn't wipe his face.

Then, with effortless calm, he pulled up his damp sleeve and wiped his eyes. There was no humiliation on his face, no self-pity—only a sharp, reckless smirk playing at the edges of his lips.

Gazing at the receding tail-lights of the packed bus, he bared his teeth in amusement.

"Bravo, BEST. Left a thousand-crore R&D lab to fetch my own prototype parts, and Mumbai welcomes me with raw gutter water. Classic civic infrastructure."

The bus vanished into the gloom without tapping the brakes, leaving behind a cloud of black diesel smoke.

Neel tossed his head, scattering dirty water droplets. 

"Brilliant, Mumbai. Rain is free, sewage showers are free... but getting home apparently requires a premium subscription."

His Italian leather sneakers were completely flooded.

It was 7:30 PM. The amber glow of bridge sodium lamps cast a sickly orange haze through sheets of rain.

Then, a sudden hollow ache gripped his stomach.

Between writing neural compilation scripts and fine-tuning open-source weights in his Worli Sea Face penthouse lab since sunrise, he’d completely forgotten to eat.

On the corner of the pedestrian walkway, a roadside food stall huddled beneath a blue plastic tarp. The rich aroma of bubbling hot oil, chickpea batter, and blistered green chilies cut straight through the stench of sewage.

Cardboard scrap scrawled with black marker: **Vada Pav — ₹15**.

Neel stepped up to the counter. "Two vada pavs to go, boss. Extra green chutney."

The vendor scooped two golden fritters from the boiling vat without looking up. "You got cash, kid? Rain knocked the cell towers out. Network's dead, UPI is down."

Neel grinned, slid his wallet from his jeans, and dropped a heavy, matte-black titanium card onto the wooden counter—**The Centurion Black Card**.

Daily pocket allowance limit: ₹1,00,000.

The vendor stared at the gleaming black metal, looked up at Neel's mud-splattered face, and burst into a hearty laugh. "You joking, boss? Where am I supposed to swipe that in this flood? No machine here. Give me paper notes!"

Neel pulled out his phone—a custom titanium prototype smartphone. The display was dead black. 0% battery.

He dug his hand deep into his jeans pocket.

His fingertips brushed against cold, damp paper and a single steel disc.

One crumpled, soaked ₹10 note. One ₹1 steel coin. The literal only loose change he had left from buying a cutting chai hours earlier.

Total cash in pocket: ₹11.

The Vada Pav was ₹15. Exactly four rupees short.

Neel pulled his empty hand out, slid the Black Card back into his pocket, and let out an incredulous laugh.

"Brilliant," he muttered into the roar of the rain. "Thousands of crores in the family empire, a Black Card with a one-lakh daily limit in my pocket, and I'm four rupees short of fried dough because this city's network died in three inches of water. If Uncle Kani or that arrogant Sunny saw this, they'd never let me live it down in the boardroom."

Neel. Nineteen years old, sole heir.

Three years ago, when a horrific highway collision took the lives of his father Aarav Gadora and mother Suman Gadora, billions in equity, shipping docks, and tech patents transferred directly into Neel’s name. His uncle Kani Gadora and aunt Cheri Gadora ran the sprawling corporate empire. They pampered Neel and catered to his every whim—deliberately keeping him comfortable so he’d stay locked in his lab and never demand an audit of the family books. Their son Sunny strutted around like a corporate crown prince.

Neel couldn't have cared less about their boardroom posturing.

His true obsession was his private high-tech laboratory. The place where he had spent months importing black-market silicon to self-implant an experimental direct neural chip behind his right temporal bone—**GENI (Gemma-4 Neural Interface)**. His personal, in-skull audio assistant.

Neel turned away from the food cart and stepped back into the flooded street.

"No matter, GENI," Neel whispered, tapping the skin behind his ear. "We'll order seafood pasta the moment I reach home."

He took three strides through the swirling black water.

Beneath the opaque surface, the heavy cast-iron manhole cover had been dislodged by hydrostatic pressure.

Neel’s right foot stepped into empty air.

*WHOOSH!*

There was no concrete pipe below. No brick walls. No sewer line.

The moment the sole of his sneaker crossed the threshold of that black water, gravity snapped like dry kindling.

The street, the diesel fumes, the blaring horns, the amber streetlights—everything vanished into a silent, bottomless chasm.

Neel plummeted.

---

He did not crash into a drain.

All around him was a colossal, churning cosmic vortex of blinding white radiance. A bottomless celestial cataract with no ceiling and no floor.

**The Celestial Cataract...**

There was no air to breathe.

And then, amidst that terrifying drag, an unimaginable, bone-crushing cosmic pressure slammed squarely against his mortal frame!

The agony was so horrific and absolute that mortal consciousness could not endure it for a single second. It felt as though every fiber of his being was being ripped apart at the seams.

Behind his right ear, the embedded silicon chip flared with searing, blinding heat...

Total darkness rushed across Neel’s eyes.

His mind shut down.

Reaching the breaking point of physical pain, he blacked out completely.

A total, absolute blackout.

He remembered nothing after that... neither what happened to him within that churning milky void, nor how many fathoms he fell into the infinite abyss.

---

Within the dead silence of the administrative void between realms, a single cold cosmic iron stamp slammed onto that unconscious drifting soul:

*`[ADMINISTRATIVE CLASSIFICATION: ROUTE B DRIFT]`*  
*`[STATUS: RIKTA / UNINDEXED]`*  
*`[DESTINY: OPEN GLITCH]`*

---

When consciousness finally flickered back, he had no idea how much time had passed.

After drowning in endless darkness, the very first sensation that violently registered...

Was the bone-jarring, brutal crash of landing face-down upon a mound of wet, rotting hay!

And the suffocating stench of sulfur, ash, and burning coal filling his throat.

The torrential Mumbai rain was gone.

The opulent Worli penthouse was lost to another world.

Earth’s nineteen-year-old billionaire prodigy... knocked out cold, had crash-landed onto a heap of refuse in an alien and brutal realm.

And he had not the faintest clue how he had survived to get here!
