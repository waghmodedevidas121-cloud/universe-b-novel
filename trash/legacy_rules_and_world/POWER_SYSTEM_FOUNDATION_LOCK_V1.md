0. PURPOSE AND NON-NEGOTIABLE METHOD
============================================================

This document establishes the research and design rules for the novel's Power System. It is not an exhaustive catalogue of Hindu religious concepts, supernatural accounts, or scriptural abilities.

The project draws inspiration from Hindu traditions while remaining an independent fictional universe. It must not claim that the novel's invented organization is an official Hindu doctrine.

CORE RULE:
First establish what a source text or tradition actually says. Only then decide whether and how that concept can be represented in the novel.

Every entry must keep four layers separate:
1. SOURCE: the primary text, passage, translation, commentary, and tradition.
2. SOURCE INTERPRETATION: what can reasonably be said from that source, including ambiguity.
3. NOVEL DESIGN: the fictional representation proposed for Universe B.
4. STATUS: approved canon, source-supported observation, proposal, or open question.

Do not convert a modern fantasy trope into “Hindu scripture says this” without a clear textual basis.

2. ALREADY APPROVED NOVEL CANON
============================================================

2.1 The fictional setting
[CANON] The novel's independent divergent setting is Universe B.
[CANON] Universe A, the mainstream/traditional Hindu mythology universe, remains untouched. Universe B's custom systems must not be retroactively imposed on Universe A.
[CANON] Universe B has its own cosmic geography, power system, soul administration, and mysteries.

2.2 The six-pillar organization
[CANON] The novel organizes its Power System under these six headings:
1. Authority
2. Energy
3. Karma
4. Tapasya
5. Boons
6. Atma-tattva — the Secret Sixth Pillar

[CANON] These are the novel's organizational headings. They are NOT to be described as an officially recognized, universal six-pillar doctrine of Hinduism.

2.3 Atma-tattva
[CANON] Atma-tattva is the novel's Secret Sixth Pillar.
[CANON] The user's cosmology establishes that Atma is not created or destroyed.
[CANON] Atma-tattva is foundational to the fictional universe, but its exact mechanics, access conditions, effects, and narrative reveal remain undisclosed/open.
[CANON] Do not reduce Atma-tattva to a routine mana bar, energy reserve, skill tree, or ordinary combat resource.
[CAUTION] The Bhagavad Gita 2.20 describes the Self as unborn, eternal, and not killed when the body is killed. This is a relevant source, but Vedanta traditions differ on how Atman relates to Brahman and on other metaphysical questions. Do not present all Hindu schools as identical.

2.4 Other established framework principles
[CANON] Source fidelity and fictional adaptation must remain visibly distinct.
[CANON] Power, authority, spiritual purity, morality, knowledge, and social rank are separate dimensions; one must not automatically imply another.
[CANON] The five original pillar headings are a framework for organizing material, not proof that every source concept belongs exclusively to one pillar.
[CANON] No new ability, limitation, ranking, or interaction becomes canon merely because it appears in a research draft.

3. THE SIX PILLARS — SAFE FOUNDATION DEFINITIONS
============================================================

These are working organizational definitions for the novel, not claims that Hinduism defines these as a single formal system.

PILLAR 1 — AUTHORITY
[PROPOSAL / WORKING SCOPE]
Use this category to document legitimate or attributed authority: divine sovereignty, cosmic office, rulership, jurisdiction, command, and the authority associated with a role or boon where a source supports it.
Do not assume that a high office automatically means the strongest combat capability.
Do not assume every use of the English word “authority” has one direct Sanskrit equivalent.
OPEN: Define which kinds of authority exist in Universe B and how their jurisdiction is limited.

PILLAR 2 — ENERGY
[PROPOSAL / WORKING SCOPE]
Use this category to research source-specific ideas such as tejas, tapas as heat/ardour in particular contexts, prana, shakti, and other relevant terms without treating them as interchangeable.
Each Sanskrit term must be recorded in its own context. A term may have different meanings in different texts and traditions.
Do not turn every term into a universal measurable energy bar unless that is explicitly marked as a fictional invention.
OPEN: Decide whether Universe B has any measurable energy model at all, and if so, which parts are purely fictional.

PILLAR 3 — KARMA
[PROPOSAL / WORKING SCOPE]
Research action, intention, consequence, ritual action, ethical action, and rebirth-related teachings as they occur in their respective sources.
Do not reduce karma to a simple good-points/bad-points score.
Do not claim every Hindu tradition describes karma, agency, grace, liberation, or rebirth in exactly the same way.
OPEN: Universe B's existing Soul Administration uses karma-related concepts. Its mechanics must be documented separately from scriptural explanations.

PILLAR 4 — TAPASYA
[SOURCE-SUPPORTED GENERAL NOTE]
Tapas/tapasya can refer to austerity, disciplined practice, ardour, or other context-specific ideas. Bhagavad Gita 17.14–17 distinguishes bodily, verbal, and mental austerity and discusses qualities/motives of practice.
Do not define tapasya only as “training to gain power.” A source may present discipline as ethical, devotional, ritual, contemplative, liberative, or power-related depending on context.
OPEN: Which practices can affect abilities in Universe B, and what fictional costs or limits apply?

PILLAR 5 — BOONS
[PROPOSAL / WORKING SCOPE]
Research source-specific accounts of boons, vows, divine grants, conditions, and consequences in the Itihasas and Puranas, among other sources.
Do not assume every boon follows one universal rule, has identical wording, or can be ranked on one scale.
For each narrative, record who grants the boon, what is actually granted, conditions, loopholes if explicitly present, and the story's consequences.
OPEN: Universe B's rules for granting, interpreting, limiting, or challenging boons remain to be designed.

PILLAR 6 — ATMA-TATTVA (SECRET SIXTH PILLAR)
[CANON: SECRET PILLAR; MECHANICS OPEN]
Atma-tattva is a foundational secret in the novel's fictional cosmology.
The source study must distinguish:
- Atman / Atma in a particular source;
- Jiva or individual living being, where the source uses that concept;
- Brahman and the Atman–Brahman relationship in specific philosophical schools;
- body, mind, senses, prana, and consciousness as discussed by different sources.
Do not assume these terms are synonyms.
Do not infer that the Atman is a manufactured object, a fuel tank, or a destructible combat item.
Do not invent a “sixth pillar” claim and attribute it to Hinduism as a whole.
OPEN: All fictional access rules, effects, signs, and reveal timing remain unapproved.

4. SOURCE RESEARCH PROTOCOL
============================================================

4.1 Primary-source priority
Use, where available:
- Sanskrit source text;
- a reputable translation;
- traditional commentary or bhashya where relevant;
- scholarly reference work for historical and philosophical context.

A translation is an interpretation, not a substitute for checking the passage and its context.

4.2 Record the exact source
Every researched entry should include:
- Text and section / chapter / verse / sutra;
- Sanskrit term(s) in transliteration and Devanagari where practical;
- a short excerpt only when needed and legally appropriate;
- a faithful summary of the passage;
- context within the narrative or argument;
- translation used and translator;
- relevant commentary or school;
- what the source does NOT establish;
- possible novel use, explicitly labelled as fiction;
- confidence and status label.

4.3 Tradition differences
Do not collapse Vedic ritual traditions, Upanishadic teachings, Vedanta schools, Yoga, Samkhya, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, Puranic narratives, Shaiva, Vaishnava, Shakta, Smarta, and Agamic/Tantric traditions into a single uniform doctrine.
The list above is a research map, not a claim that these categories are mutually exclusive or exhaustive.
When two traditions disagree, record the disagreement. Do not force a false synthesis just to simplify the novel's system.

4.4 Historical care
Do not assume every text is from the same period or that later practices existed in identical form in the Vedas.
Tantric traditions are diverse and historically complex; they must not be reduced to one stereotype or treated as a single universal manual.

4.5 No unsupported mechanics
A scriptural account of a siddhi, weapon, mantra, boon, deity, or cosmological event does not automatically establish:
- a game-like cooldown;
- a numeric power level;
- a mana cost;
- universal access conditions;
- a universal counter;
- a single hierarchy across all beings;
- a universal energy source.
Any such mechanic is a novel proposal unless a specific source clearly supports the exact claim.

5. INITIAL SOURCE-SUPPORTED ANCHORS
============================================================

These anchors are starting points for research, not the complete research result.

A. Bhagavad Gita 2.20 — Atman and bodily death
Sanskrit opening:
“na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin ...”
Devenagari:
“न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः।
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे॥”
Careful summary: The verse describes the Self as unborn, eternal, enduring, and not killed when the body is killed.
What it does not by itself establish: a fictional combat ability, a power ranking, a universal soul-administration workflow, or that every Vedanta school explains the Self in exactly the same way.
Reference: Gita Supersite, IIT Kanpur
https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&ecsiva=1&etgb=1&etssa=1&field_chapter_value=2&field_nsutra_value=20&httyn=1&language=dv

B. Bhagavad Gita 3.27 — Prakriti, gunas, and doership
Sanskrit:
“प्रकृतेः क्रियमाणानि गुणैः कर्माणि सर्वशः।
अहङ्कारविमूढात्मा कर्ताऽहमिति मन्यते॥”
Careful summary: The verse says actions are performed by the gunas of prakriti, while one deluded by ego thinks “I am the doer.” Interpretations need their commentarial context.
What it does not by itself establish: that the gunas are three combat elements, a character alignment chart, or a direct power-meter.
Reference: Gita Supersite, IIT Kanpur
https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&ecsiva=1&etgb=1&etsiva=1&field_chapter_value=3&field_nsutra_value=27&language=dv&scsh=1

C. Bhagavad Gita 17.14–17.16 — Tapas
Gita 17.14 identifies bodily austerity through practices including reverence, purity, straightforwardness, brahmacharya, and non-injury. Verses 17.15–16 address verbal and mental austerity.
Careful summary: Tapas is not presented only as a battle-training technique; bodily, verbal, and mental dimensions are discussed.
What it does not by itself establish: a universal mechanism where tapasya directly converts into measurable combat power.
Reference: Gita Supersite, IIT Kanpur
https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?etgb=1&field_chapter_value=17&field_nsutra_value=14&language=dv&scsh=1&setgb=1

D. Patanjali Yoga Sutras, Vibhuti Pada (Chapter 3)
The chapter discusses samyama and various extraordinary attainments; the meaning and status of these passages require the sutra context and commentary. Yoga Sutra 3.37 is often translated as treating certain attainments as obstacles in relation to samadhi, despite being powers in an outward-facing context.
Careful summary: Extraordinary attainments are not simply equivalent to spiritual liberation; interpretation depends on the text and commentary.
What it does not establish: that every Hindu tradition accepts a single list or ranking of siddhis.
Research links:
https://www.swaveda.com/texts/yoga-sutras/3/
https://mouseia.org/texts/yoga-sutras/book-3

E. Vedanta and the Atman–Brahman question
Different Vedanta schools interpret the relationship between Atman and Brahman differently. Advaita Vedanta associated with Shankara teaches non-dual identity in a specific philosophical framework; this must not be universalized to all Hindu traditions.
Scholarly reference:
https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/shankara/

F. Agamas and Tantras
Agamic and Tantric traditions are diverse in doctrine, ritual, initiation, mantra, visualization, deity practice, and philosophical orientation. Avoid treating “Tantra” as one single power system.
Scholarly reference:
https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62249/chapter-abstract/551403085
https://academic.oup.com/reference/62357/reference-article-abstract/554520421

G. Astras, boons, and epic narratives
Ramayana and Mahabharata narratives include divine weapons and extraordinary grants, but each episode must be examined individually. Record the exact narrative, conditions, users, and outcome instead of creating one universal rule from selected examples.
Academic starting point for astras:
https://ir.canterbury.ac.nz/items/0a90d45e-34b7-47c3-ab19-a7d9a609198c

H. Karma, desire, and rebirth
Upanishadic passages such as Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4 and Chandogya Upanishad 5.10 are relevant research targets for action, desire, conduct, and rebirth. Exact claims must be checked against the chosen translation and commentarial context before they are used in the novel.
Status: Research target; no novel mechanics approved from these passages yet.

6. CROSS-PILLAR CLASSIFICATION RULE
============================================================

A source concept may relate to more than one pillar, or may not fit cleanly into any one pillar.
Do not force exclusive assignment.

For each entry, use:
- Primary topic;
- Secondary links;
- source-specific meaning;
- possible fictional mapping;
- whether the mapping changes the source meaning;
- unresolved implications.

Example:
A boon granted after tapasya may connect to both TAPASYA and BOONS in the novel's index. This does not mean the source itself presents a six-pillar chart.

============================================================
7. DIMENSIONS THAT MUST NOT BE CONFUSED
============================================================

Keep these dimensions separate in the novel's documentation:
- combat capability;
- cosmic / administrative authority;
- spiritual purity;
- ethical conduct;
- knowledge;
- ritual status;
- social rank;
- liberation or spiritual attainment;
- access to a particular ability.

A being can be strong in one dimension without automatically being highest in all others. Any relationship between dimensions must be justified by the specific source or marked as a fictional rule.

============================================================
8. UNIVERSE B IMPLEMENTATION FIREWALL
============================================================

Universe B may use a fictional rule inspired by a source, but the document must label it as fiction.

For every adaptation, ask:
1. What does the source actually say?
2. What is the passage's narrative, ritual, or philosophical context?
3. Is the interpretation specific to one school or tradition?
4. What element is being adapted into Universe B?
5. What is being added by the novel?
6. Could the wording falsely imply that the fictional rule is Hindu doctrine?
7. Does the new rule contradict previously approved Universe B canon?
8. Does it accidentally impose Universe B rules on Universe A?

If the fictional mechanic is new, write: “Novel-specific adaptation; not asserted as a universal scriptural rule.”
