## 1. Category 1: Classical Narrative Theory & Structural Poetics

This category establishes macro-narrative coherence, causal sequencing, dramatic value shifts, and story architecture.

### 1.1 Robert McKee — *Story: Substance, Structure, Style, and the Principles of Screenwriting* (1997)
- **Central Argument:** A story is not a random sequence of events; it is a meaningful progression of scenes where each scene produces a measurable **value charge shift** (e.g., from Hope to Despair, Trust to Betrayal, Vulnerability to Power) through progressive complications and conflict.
- **Key Mechanics:** The Law of Conflict, The Gap (expectation vs. reality), Turning Points, Beat-Level Beats vs. Scene Climax.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Prevents "flat scenes" where characters simply converse without altering their emotional, political, or tactical status.

### 1.2 John Truby — *The Anatomy of Story: 22 Steps to Becoming a Master Storyteller* (2007)
- **Central Argument:** Story design must be organic rather than mechanical. Character growth and plot structure are identical: the protagonist's internal moral weakness/psychological need must directly dictate the external opponent and the battleground.
- **Key Mechanics:** The 7 Major Story Steps (Weakness/Need, Desire, Opponent, Plan, Battle, Self-Revelation, New Equilibrium), The Character Web.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Ensures that characters in the 120-person roster act from genuine sociological and philosophical friction rather than functioning as artificial obstacles.

### 1.3 Dwight V. Swain — *Techniques of the Selling Writer* (1965)
- **Central Argument:** Fiction operates on microscopic psychomotor stimulus-response patterns. Long-form narrative tension is generated by alternating between goal-driven **Scenes** (Goal → Conflict → Disaster) and reflective **Sequels** (Reaction → Dilemma → Decision).
- **Key Mechanics:** Motivation-Reaction Units (MRUs), Cause-Effect Sensory Order, The 3-Part Scene Disaster.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Provides the foundational micro-architecture for the upcoming *Scene Builder* and *Liveliness Diagnostic*.

### 1.4 Larry Brooks — *Story Engineering* (2011) & *Story Physics* (2013)
- **Central Argument:** Compelling storytelling relies on six core competencies: Concept, Character, Theme, Structure, Scene Execution, and Writing Voice. Structural milestones must create irreversible shifts in dramatic physics.
- **Key Mechanics:** The First Plot Point (Point of No Return), Midpoint Context Shift (from Wanderer to Warrior), Pinch Points.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Dictates Volume-level milestone placement and ensures the Narrative Veil in Volumes 1–3 escalates without premature cosmic reveals.

## 2. Category 2: Scene Construction, Liveliness & Prose Craft

This category focuses on creating a vivid, continuous, sensory "fictive dream" in the reader's imagination.

### 2.1 John Gardner — *The Art of Fiction: Notes on Craft for Young Writers* (1983)
- **Central Argument:** The primary task of fiction is to induce and sustain a **vivid and continuous fictional dream**. Any technical lapse—awkward phrasing, factual inconsistency, careless abstraction, or ungrounded action—shatters this dream and reminds the reader they are looking at ink on a page.
- **Key Mechanics:** Concrete Detail vs. Abstract Description, Psychic Distance, Faults of Manner (sentimentality, frigidity).
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Mandates the physical grounding of every scene (spatial orientation, body weight, posture, tactile reality).

### 2.2 Sandra Scofield — *The Scene Book: A Primer for the Fiction Writer* (2007)
- **Central Argument:** A scene is an event that takes place in specific time and space, driven by purpose and sensory immediacy, leaving characters in a changed psychological state.
- **Key Mechanics:** The Pulse of a Scene, Scene Centers, Staging and Blocking, The Five Essential Scene Components.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Forms the core benchmark for testing whether a drafted chapter segment qualifies as a true scene or mere filler exposition.

### 2.3 Donald Maass — *The Fire in Fiction* (2009) & *Writing the Breakout Novel* (2001)
- **Central Argument:** Readers do not connect to events; they connect to the **intensity of character interiority** and high personal stakes. Every scene must carry emotional micro-tension, even quiet dialogue scenes.
- **Key Mechanics:** Micro-Tension on every page, Inner Conflict Layering, Third-Dimension Characterization.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Provides the techniques needed to prevent dialogue from sounding like dry exposition or tactical lore dumps.

### 2.4 Ursula K. Le Guin — *Steering the Craft: A 21st-Century Guide to Sailing the Sea of Story* (2015)
- **Central Argument:** Prose is acoustic and musical before it is intellectual. Sentence length, cadence, punctuation rhythm, and point-of-view discipline create subconscious reader momentum.
- **Key Mechanics:** The Sound of Prose, Cadence and Pacing, POV Hopping vs. Disciplined Third-Person Limited.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Directly informs the dual-track English/Hindi translation rhythm, ensuring both editions maintain acoustic bite and narrative flow.

### 2.5 Gary Provost — *Make Every Word Count* (1980) & *100 Ways to Improve Your Writing* (1985)
- **Central Argument:** The rhythm of sentences dictates the emotional tension of the reader. Monotonous sentence length creates mental numbness; varied sentence music creates hypnotic immersion.
- **Key Mechanics:** The famous "Music of Words" principle (blending five-word sentences with cascading rhythmic prose).
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Establishes the concrete sentence-editing guidelines for high-stakes combat scenes in the Central City Sand-Pits.

## 3. Category 3: Cognitive Science, Neurobiology & Reader Immersion

This category investigates the psychological mechanisms behind attention, comprehension, and emotional transport.

### 3.1 Rolf A. Zwaan & Gabriel A. Radvansky — *Situation Models in Narrative Comprehension* (Psychological Bulletin, 1998)
- **Documented Finding:** Readers do not merely parse words; they construct an active multidimensional **Situation Model** in working memory along five dimensions: **Time, Space, Protagonist, Causality, and Intentionality**. When any dimension drops out (e.g., forgotten spatial layout), cognitive friction spikes and reader immersion collapses.
- **Application:** Explains why "white room syndrome" (talking heads in an ungrounded void) causes immediate reader disengagement.

### 3.2 Jeffrey M. Zacks et al. — *Event Segmentation Theory* (Psychological Bulletin, 2007)
- **Documented Finding:** The human brain continuously segments ongoing streams of activity into discrete "events" based on prediction boundaries. A new event begins when sensory features change (movement to a new room, arrival of a new character, sudden sound).
- **Application:** Establishes precise biological rules for where scene transitions and paragraph breaks should occur.

### 3.3 Raymond A. Mar — *The Neural Bases of Social Cognition and Story Comprehension* (Annual Review of Psychology, 2011)
- **Documented Finding:** Brain imaging reveals that reading narrative prose activates the same neural networks (mirror neurons, theory of mind networks) used during real-world social interaction and physical movement. Concrete motor verbs ("he shoved", "she gripped") activate the motor cortex, whereas abstract summaries ("he was aggressive") activate only linguistic processing centers.
- **Application:** Proves scientifically why concrete, sensory-grounded prose creates vastly superior emotional immersion compared to abstract adjectives.

### 3.4 Lisa Feldman Barrett — *How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain* (2017)
- **Documented Finding:** Emotions are not hardwired universal triggers; they are predictive conceptual categories constructed from internal visceral sensations (interoception) combined with external situational context.
- **Application:** Demonstrates why simply naming an emotion ("he felt angry") fails to move a reader, whereas rendering physical interoception (clenched jaw, dry throat, narrowed peripheral vision) coupled with situational frustration triggers genuine empathetic emotion.

## 4. Category 4: Serial Fiction, Web Novel Systems & Master Author Craft

This category examines long-running chapter retention, serial cliffhangers, and master author techniques.

### 4.1 Brandon Sanderson — *Lectures on Creative Writing* (BYU, 2020)
- **Documented Methodology:** Storytelling operates on **Promises, Progress, and Payoff**. An author establishes clear narrative promises early, provides visible and satisfying micro-milestones (progress), and delivers earned, logical resolutions (payoffs).
- **Key Concepts:** Sanderson’s Laws of Magic (limitations > powers), The Pacing of Revelations.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Directly guides the 3-Volume Narrative Veil and tournament progression arcs.

### 4.2 Stephen King — *On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft* (2000)
- **Documented Methodology:** Storytelling as fossil excavation; rejection of plot-driven contrivance in favor of placing believable characters into authentic situations and observing their reactions. Intense hostility toward adverb-heavy prose and passive voice.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Grounds character reactions during sudden, brutal combat crises under the *Kruti Law*.

### 4.3 Cuttlefish That Loves Diving (爱潜水的乌贼) — *Lord of the Mysteries Author Notes & Retrospectives* (2018–2020)
- **Documented Web Novel Craft:** Master of micro-suspense, Victorian/mythic mystery layering, and pacing 1,400+ serialized chapters.
- **Key Concepts:** The "Acting Method" (character advancement integrated into roleplay and mental anchor preservation), Information Asymmetry between protagonist and cosmic entities, Arc Climax Convergence (all loose threads converge into one cataclysmic 5-chapter finale).
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Provides direct architectural patterns for balancing the 120-character roster and managing the gradual unpeeling of the Tier 4 Corrupted Realm.

### 4.4 Hajime Isayama & Kentaro Miura — Visual Tension & Information Withholding
- **Craft Observation:** Masters of high-stakes, physically devastating conflicts combined with deep sociological and existential dread.
- **Key Concepts:** Perspective Shifting (revealing the same event from the opposing faction's vantage point to invert moral assumptions), Unforgiving Physical Consequence (permanent injuries, environmental destruction), Visual Anchor Pacing.
- **Relevance to Universe B:** Essential for staging the Sand-Pit tournament battles and the brutal reality of the Great Setu vanguard.
