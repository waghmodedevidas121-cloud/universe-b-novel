## 1. Cosmic Nature & The Imperial Office

Indra is **not** an eternal individual name; it is a **constitutional office** (*Pada*). The reigning Indra holds the throne of Svarga for one Manvantara (71 Mahāyugas).

- **Condition of Attainment:** Any mortal, asura, or king who successfully conducts **100 Aśvamedha Yajñas** (horse sacrifices) without flaw legally earns the right to depose the current Indra and claim the throne.
- **The Chronic Insecurity (Indrāsana-Bhaya):**
  - Because his throne is legally contestable, Indra is permanently paranoid.
  - Whenever a mortal or asura engages in severe Tapasya, Indra fears it is an insurrection against his crown.
  - *Standard Counter-Strategy:* He dispatches celestial nymphs (Apsarās like Menakā, Rambhā) or disrupts rituals through psychological and weather attacks.

---

## 2. Role in this Novel: The Celestial Establishment & Shadow Politics

1. **The Political Antagonist / Ambiguous Force:**
   - Indra is not evil, but he is fiercely protective of his divine monopoly and celestial privilege.
   - In a modern world where humans begin tapping into ancient Prāṇa, Nadis, and Astras, Indra’s informants (modern secret societies aligned with Svarga) view mortal empowerment as an existential threat.
2. **Divine Resource Gatekeeping:**
   - He controls access to celestial realms, Soma/Amrita distillations, and atmospheric astras. 
   - Mortals who bypass the traditional priestly hierarchy to awaken direct divine authority through intense Tapasya draw immediate scrutiny from Indra’s agents.
