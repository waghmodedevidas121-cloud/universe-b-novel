4. SIDDHI, TAPASYA, MANTRA, ASTRA, BOON — DIFFERENCES
======================================================================

Ya terms la ekach power type samju naye.

SIDDHI: - Siddhi mhanje context nusar accomplishment, attainment kiwa
extraordinary capability. - Yoga Sutras madhye siddhi sambandhit varnan
aahe; tyanchi interpretation ani spiritual significance
source/commentary nusar tapasavi.

TAPASYA: - Practice, discipline, austerity kiwa spiritual exertion
sambandhit concept. - Tapasya hi siddhi nahi; kahi kathांमध्ये tapasya
nantar boon kiwa extraordinary result yeto, pan te pratyek veles
automatic formula nahi.

MANTRA: - Sacred utterance/practice, ritual and spiritual context. -
Mantra ha sarva granthanmadhe “spell” cha exact equivalent nahi.

ASTRA: - Epic/Puranic context madhil divine weapon concept. - Astra,
shastra, mantra ani boon ya ekach category nahit. Specific text madhil
relation nondava.

BOON / VARADANA: - Divine gift or granted benefit in a narrative
context. - Boon he sadhana, siddhi, mantra kiwa weapon yanchyashi
related asu shakto, pan tyanchi vyakhya ekach nahi.

ATMA-TATTVA: - Atman/self chya tattvashi sambandhit philosophical
concept. - Aaplya novel madhye Secret Sixth Pillar; pan tyala ek normal
ability kiwa resource samju naye.

6. POWER, AUTHORITY, PURITY, AND MORALITY
======================================================================

These concepts must remain distinct in the novel’s documentation:

COMBAT CAPABILITY: A character’s demonstrated ability in a particular
encounter.

AUTHORITY: A right, role, jurisdiction, command, or protection described
in the relevant fictional or textual context.

SOUL PURITY: A spiritual concept that must be described through the
chosen tradition’s own framework.

MORALITY / DHARMA: Ethical, religious, social, and cosmic duties or
principles; definitions depend on context and tradition.

SPIRITUAL ATTAINMENT: A term for the outcome of a path or practice in a
specific tradition; it should not automatically be translated into
combat rank.

KNOWLEDGE: Understanding of a mantra, weapon, ritual, doctrine, or
ability; knowledge alone does not automatically mean authority or
successful use.

No one dimension should automatically decide every battle or every
social role. This is a narrative guideline, not a claim that all Hindu
traditions share a single rank system.

======================================================================

Use primary texts and reputable translations/commentaries where
possible. Record exact chapter/verse and the translation/commentary
used. Do not rely on unsourced social-media lists for technical claims.

A. BHAGAVAD GITA - 2.20: Atman described as unborn, eternal, and not
destroyed when the body is destroyed. - 3.27: Actions, Prakriti, Gunas,
and the sense of doership. - 17.14–17: Forms of Tapas associated with
body, speech, and mind; classification by qualities in context.
Reference: Gita Supersite, IIT Kanpur.
https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/

B. PATANJALI YOGA SUTRAS - Vibhuti Pada, Chapter 3: Samyama and
descriptions associated with siddhis. - Interpret the passages in their
full philosophical context; do not treat each passage as a standalone
game ability. Reference editions/translations should be compared where
possible. Possible text access:
https://www.swaveda.com/texts/yoga-sutras/3/
https://mouseia.org/texts/yoga-sutras/book-3

C. UPANISHADS AND VEDANTA - Atman, Brahman, consciousness, liberation,
and their relationship. - Interpretations differ across philosophical
schools; distinguish Advaita from other Vedanta interpretations.
Reference overview: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, entry on
Shankara: https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/shankara/

D. RAMAYANA AND MAHABHARATA - Divya astras, boons, vows, tapasya, divine
intervention, dharma, and consequences. - Record the exact
version/edition used, since textual traditions and recensions can

https://ir.canterbury.ac.nz/items/0a90d45e-34b7-47c3-ab19-a7d9a609198c

E. PURANAS, AGAMAS, AND TANTRIC TRADITIONS - Deity-specific cosmologies,

https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62249/chapter-abstract/551403085
