======================================================================
0. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS
======================================================================

This document turns the previously approved six-pillar framework into one coherent, usable fictional system. The mechanical rules below are NOVEL DESIGN, not claims that Hindu scriptures teach a six-pillar game-like system.

Status labels:
[ESTABLISHED CANON] Already specified by the author.
[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED] A complete rule proposed here to make the system operational. It becomes immutable only when the author approves it.
[OPEN SECRET] Deliberately withheld from characters/readers; not a missing design accident.
[OPEN AUTHOR CHOICE] Must remain undecided until the story needs it.
[SOURCE CONTEXT] A scriptural/philosophical concept used as inspiration, not proof of a fictional rule.
[CAUTION] Do not claim this fictional rule is a universal Hindu doctrine.

Core boundary:
- Universe A, the mainstream/traditional Hindu mythology universe, remains untouched.
- Universe B is an independent fictional universe inspired by selected Hindu texts, traditions, and philosophical questions.
- The six pillars are the novel's design taxonomy, not an established universal Hindu doctrine.
- A source narrative may inspire a mechanic, but it does not automatically prove that mechanic.

======================================================================
1. THE SYSTEM AT A GLANCE
======================================================================

The Six Pillars:
1. AUTHORITY — what a being/office is legitimately permitted to command, govern, or affect.
2. ENERGY — the capacity and medium through which an action is sustained or expressed.
3. KARMA — accumulated causal obligations, consequences, and unresolved results attached to action.
4. TAPASYA — disciplined transformation through sustained practice, restraint, concentration, and sacrifice.
5. BOONS — specific conditional grants that create a defined exception, privilege, protection, or capability.
6. ATMA-TATTVA — the Secret Sixth Pillar; the deepest identity/continuity principle, not a fuel or combat stat.

The pillars are not six colours of the same energy. They answer different questions:
- Authority: “Are you entitled to do this here?”
- Energy: “Can you sustain or express the action?”
- Karma: “What causal obligations and consequences are involved?”
- Tapasya: “What have you transformed or become capable of through disciplined practice?”
- Boon: “What specific exception or capability has been granted, and under what conditions?”
- Atma-tattva: “What is the underlying continuity/identity that the ordinary system cannot fully quantify?” Its operational truth is a narrative secret.

No pillar automatically implies moral goodness, social rank, wisdom, spiritual purity, or victory.

======================================================================
2. UNIVERSAL RULES
======================================================================

RULE 1 — NO FREE EFFECT
Every non-trivial effect must have a valid basis: capability, authority, condition, preparation, or a clearly established exceptional rule. “Because the plot needs it” is not an in-world explanation.

RULE 2 — POWER IS NOT ONE NUMBER
There is no single universal power level that accurately measures every being. A character may have high Energy but little Authority; high Authority but low personal combat skill; a powerful Boon with a narrow condition; or deep Tapasya without a large destructive output.

RULE 3 — ACCESS, CAPACITY, CONTROL, AND CONSEQUENCE ARE SEPARATE
Having access to a power does not mean having enough capacity to use it. Capacity does not guarantee control. Control does not remove consequences.

RULE 4 — KNOWLEDGE IS NOT OWNERSHIP
Knowing a mantra, weapon name, ritual pattern, or theory does not automatically grant the right, capacity, initiation, compatibility, or control needed to use it.

RULE 5 — EXCEPTIONS MUST BE SPECIFIC
Boons, curses, offices, and special cases do not create universal rules unless the story explicitly establishes them as such.

RULE 6 — NO AUTOMATIC MORAL SCORING
A virtuous character is not automatically the strongest. A morally compromised character is not automatically weak. Karma is causal and consequential, not a simple “good points/bad points” scoreboard.

RULE 7 — NO UNLIMITED STACKING
Two effects do not become infinitely powerful just because they are combined. Each effect keeps its conditions, scope, and limitations. Conflicts are resolved by jurisdiction, specific conditions, control, and context—not by arbitrary addition.

RULE 8 — THE SUPREME TRINITY IS NOT A NORMAL BUILD
Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva/Mahesh are established as Supreme Creator, Preserver, and Destroyer in Universe B and stand outside ordinary Power System limits. They are not merely characters with maximum stats, and the six pillars do not provide a method to manufacture or surpass their status.

RULE 9 — ANANTA IS NOT A POWER SOURCE
Ananta is a non-conscious system. It records actual changes and follows Supreme Orders. It does not choose destinies, award powers, judge souls, or independently change the rules.

RULE 10 — SOURCE AND FICTION STAY SEPARATE
A scriptural account may inspire a story case, but the novel must not present invented numerical costs, ranks, or six-pillar mechanics as scriptural facts.

3. PILLAR I — AUTHORITY
======================================================================

Core definition:
Authority is the valid scope within which a being, office, vow, grant, or established cosmic rule is entitled to issue commands or affect a domain. It is permission/jurisdiction, not raw strength.

[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED] Four Authority dimensions:
A. SOURCE — where authority comes from: cosmic office, Supreme Order, a valid Boon, a recognized oath/role, or a locally established law.
B. DOMAIN — what it applies to: a place, class of beings, duty, event, object, or specific action.
C. SCOPE — how far it reaches: target, area, duration, and kind of effect.
D. LIMIT — the boundary that prevents the authority from applying everywhere.

Operational rules:
1. Authority must have a source and a defined domain.
2. A title alone is not enough; the relevant jurisdiction must apply to the current situation.
3. Authority may permit an action without supplying the Energy, skill, or control to execute it.
4. A character may resist an authority through a valid higher/specific jurisdiction, an explicit exception, or a rule that makes the authority inapplicable. Raw anger does not automatically cancel it.
5. Two authorities in conflict are resolved in this order:
   a. explicit Supreme Order or immutable cosmological rule;
   b. the more specific valid jurisdiction over the exact target/action;
   c. a valid explicit exception or Boon condition;
   d. if still unresolved, treat it as a genuine conflict requiring a story event or adjudication—not an invented universal power score.
6. The Supreme Trinity's status is not derived from ordinary office ranks and cannot be challenged by a normal Authority build.
7. Authority can be delegated only if its source permits delegation. Delegated authority cannot exceed the scope of its source.
8. Authority is not identical to social respect, fear, spiritual purity, or combat dominance.

Failure modes:
- Out-of-domain command: no valid effect.
- Excess scope: effect stops at the valid boundary.
- Conflicting jurisdiction: action is blocked, limited, or requires resolution.
- No execution capacity: permission exists, but the user cannot carry out the action.

Examples:
- A soul administrator may have jurisdiction over a defined administrative process but not permission to rewrite the Supreme Trinity's foundational laws.
- A ruler may command an army but not automatically defeat every soldier personally.
- A Boon may grant protection from one defined class of harm without granting broad cosmic Authority.

Research inspiration:
Source-specific accounts of divine offices, vows, grants, and jurisdiction may inform individual cases. Do not claim “Authority” is one universal Sanskrit technical category.

======================================================================
4. PILLAR II — ENERGY
======================================================================

Core definition:
Energy is the fictional system's operational capacity to sustain an effect. Terms such as prana, tejas, tapas, and shakti occur in diverse textual and traditional contexts and must not be treated as interchangeable source terms. In Universe B, “Energy” is a design category, not a claim that those concepts are one substance.

[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED] Energy has four separate questions:
A. RESERVE — how much usable capacity is currently available.
B. THROUGHPUT — how quickly that capacity can be expressed.
C. CONTROL — how accurately it can be directed.
D. STABILITY — how safely it can be maintained without disruption.

Operational rules:
1. Not every being, event, or power must expose a visible Energy bar.
2. Energy is measured only where the story's institution, technique, or instrument can actually measure it. Measurement is imperfect and may be domain-specific.
3. A high reserve does not guarantee high throughput, fine control, or stability.
4. A low-cost action can be repeated; a high-throughput action may exhaust the user quickly even if the total reserve is large.
5. Energy does not by itself grant permission, change Karma, create a Boon, or rewrite Authority.
6. Energy may recover through rest, suitable environment, a defined practice, or another explicitly established method. Recovery rates are not universal unless the story sets them.
7. External energy, if introduced, must have a source, compatibility conditions, transfer method, and risk. No automatic infinite battery.
8. Using a technique beyond control or stability can cause interruption, misdirection, temporary incapacity, or other pre-established consequences. Do not use arbitrary consequences only to punish a character.
9. Do not equate emotional intensity with unlimited Energy.
10. Do not equate Prana with a simple mana bar in descriptions of real Hindu traditions.

Recommended fictional measurement:
Use qualitative bands rather than a universal numerical ranking:
- depleted
- strained
- stable
- high-output
- overload-risk
These are operational states, not spiritual rankings. Specific characters and cultures may use different terminology.

Failure modes:
- Reserve depletion: the effect cannot be sustained.
- Throughput bottleneck: the effect is weaker/slower than intended.
- Control failure: the effect misfires or loses precision.
- Stability failure: the user cannot maintain the effect safely.
- Compatibility failure: a particular source/medium cannot be used by that user.

======================================================================
5. PILLAR III — KARMA
======================================================================

Core definition:
Karma is treated as causally significant action and its consequences, including unresolved results/obligations. Universe B's implementation is fictional and must not be presented as a universal doctrinal summary of Hinduism.

[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED] Keep three ledgers conceptually separate:
A. CAUSAL DEBT/CONSEQUENCE — unresolved results or obligations connected to actions.
B. RIPENING RESULTS — consequences whose conditions for manifestation are becoming present.
C. COMPLETED RESULTS — results that have already unfolded and must not be counted again as still-pending obligations.

These are novel design labels, not exact scriptural technical categories.

Operational rules:
1. Karma is not a morality score and cannot be summed into a single “good/evil number.”
2. Intention, action, context, impact, responsibility, and the relevant tradition's interpretation may matter; no single factor should be treated as universally decisive in every case.
3. Karma does not automatically convert into Energy, combat strength, a visible aura, or a guaranteed reward.
4. A character cannot erase a consequence merely by becoming powerful. A consequence can be resolved only through the causal/narrative conditions the story establishes.
5. Karma may affect compatibility with a birth, duty, relationship, or event only when Universe B's rules explicitly connect those elements. It must not become a universal authorial excuse for every event.
6. The story should distinguish consequence from punishment. A painful outcome is not automatically proof that the person deserved it.
7. A boon or curse may alter the path or conditions through which a consequence unfolds, but it does not automatically delete all other causal obligations.
8. An event's outcome may be uncertain to characters even when causes exist. Karma is not a guarantee that characters can predict the future.
9. Karma, Atma-bhagya, and the Ananta record are distinct:
   - Karma: causal consequences/obligations.
   - Atma-bhagya: the author's term for karma-influenced fortune, especially the Kul/family into which a soul is born.
   - Ananta: a non-conscious record of actual changes, not a karmic judge or destiny-maker.
10. Do not invent an exact numerical “Karma score” unless the author deliberately wants an unreliable institution-specific proxy. Such a proxy must not be the true universal nature of Karma.

Failure modes:
- Misreading a ripening consequence as a guaranteed prophecy.
- Mistaking a fortunate outcome for proof of moral superiority.
- Trying to use power to bypass a consequence without satisfying the relevant rule.
- Administrative misclassification: a fictional institution may misunderstand a soul's situation; the universe itself is not thereby shown to be morally simplistic.

======================================================================
6. PILLAR IV — TAPASYA
======================================================================

Core definition:
Tapasya in the novel is disciplined, sustained transformation through practice, restraint, concentration, endurance, or purposeful commitment. This is a broad fictional design scope; textual meanings differ by context and tradition.

[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED] Tapasya develops capacity in four possible tracks:
A. DISCIPLINE — consistency and the ability to maintain a chosen practice.
B. CONTROL — precision, restraint, and reduced waste.
C. ENDURANCE — ability to sustain a practice or withstand its demands.
D. INSIGHT — understanding gained through practice, when the practice actually supports such insight.

These tracks are not automatically combat levels. A practice may develop one without developing the others.

Operational rules:
1. Time spent alone does not guarantee advancement. The practice must be relevant, sustained, and meaningfully performed.
2. Tapasya cannot automatically grant any desired ability. Outcomes depend on the practice, the practitioner's starting condition, insight, context, and the story's established rules.
3. Tapasya may improve Energy control or make a technique more reliable, but it does not automatically create Authority or a Boon.
4. Tapasya is not a universal “currency” that can be exchanged for anything.
5. Extreme self-denial is not automatically more effective than balanced discipline. The story must establish why a particular practice works.
6. A practice can produce narrow mastery rather than general superiority.
7. A practitioner may gain insight without gaining destructive power.
8. A disrupted practice may delay progress or reveal a weakness, but must not erase years of development arbitrarily.
9. Tapasya and abhyasa (practice) may overlap in some fictional training designs, but they are not treated as synonyms in every source or tradition.
10. No ordinary Tapasya process lets a character become equal to or replace the Supreme Trinity.

Failure modes:
- Inconsistent practice: slower or less stable development.
- Wrong method: no intended gain, or a limited gain different from what was expected.
- Misunderstanding: skill increases without correct interpretation.
- Over-specialization: excellent performance in one domain, poor transfer to another.

Recommended progression:
1. Orientation — understands the method.
2. Regularity — can repeat it consistently.
3. Control — can apply it with precision.
4. Integration — can use it under varied conditions.
5. Mastery — can adapt it while respecting its limits.
These are author-designed training stages, not scriptural universal ranks.

======================================================================
7. PILLAR V — BOONS
======================================================================

Core definition:
A Boon is a specific grant that establishes a capability, protection, exception, access right, or condition-bound effect. Epic and Puranic narratives provide varied case studies; they do not establish one uniform universal contract template.

[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED] Every major Boon must have a Boon Record with:
1. GRANTOR / SOURCE — who or what granted it.
2. BENEFICIARY — who receives it.
3. GRANTED EFFECT — exact benefit.
4. CONDITIONS — what must be true for it to operate.
5. EXCLUSIONS — what it does not protect or permit.
6. DURATION — permanent, temporary, event-bound, or otherwise specified.
7. INTERACTION — how it behaves with Authority, Energy, Karma, and other Boons.
8. INTERPRETATION RISK — any wording ambiguity that can create story consequences.

Operational rules:
1. A Boon does only what its established wording/scope supports. Do not silently broaden it.
2. A Boon is not necessarily unlimited, permanent, or invulnerable.
3. Conditions can be precise, broad, or ambiguous; the story must show why an interpretation is valid.
4. A Boon may supply a special effect without teaching the beneficiary how to use it well.
5. A Boon can protect against one kind of harm while leaving other kinds untouched.
6. Two Boons do not automatically stack. If their conditions conflict, apply their explicit terms and relevant Authority; otherwise treat the conflict as unresolved until the story decides it.
7. A Boon is not the same as a curse. A curse is a distinct narrative mechanism and should not be automatically placed under the same rules. The research catalogue may cross-reference both as conditional narrative effects.
8. A grantor's power does not mean the beneficiary inherits the grantor's full authority.
9. A Boon cannot override the Supreme Trinity or foundational rules unless the author explicitly writes a Supreme Order establishing that exception.
10. Do not reduce every boon to a loophole puzzle. Conditions should be coherent, legible, and dramatically earned.

Boon record template:
ID:
Source/Grantor:
Beneficiary:
Exact effect:
Conditions:
Exclusions:
Duration:
Known countermeasures:
Interaction with the six pillars:
Source inspiration (if any):
Novel-only elements:
Status: [PROPOSAL] / [CANON] / [OPEN]

8. PILLAR VI — ATMA-TATTVA (SECRET SIXTH PILLAR)
======================================================================

[ESTABLISHED CANON]
- Atma-tattva is the Secret Sixth Pillar.
- In the author's cosmology, Atma is not created or destroyed.
- It is foundational and must not be reduced to mana, an energy reserve, a skill tree, or an ordinary combat resource.
- Its precise mechanics, access conditions, effects, and reveal remain secret/open.

[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED NARRATIVE SAFEGUARDS]
1. Do not give Atma-tattva a visible bar, common numerical rank, routine training tree, or standard marketplace value.
2. Do not make it a stronger version of Energy.
3. Do not make every person who mentions Atman automatically awaken it.
4. Do not use it as a universal excuse that solves any conflict without setup.
5. Ordinary institutions may have incomplete, mistaken, or no measurements for it. Their inability to measure it does not make them automatically foolish.
6. Its reveal should change the reader's understanding of the other pillars or the universe, not merely add a larger attack.
7. The system must not claim that the novel's exact Atma-tattva mechanics are a settled doctrine shared by all Hindu philosophical schools.
8. Keep the difference between Atma as a philosophical/religious concept and Atma-tattva as this novel's designed sixth pillar explicit.

[OPEN SECRET — DO NOT FILL IN PREMATURELY]
- Who, if anyone, can directly perceive or interact with Atma-tattva?
- Can it be consciously accessed, or is it only revealed through rare circumstances?
- What can it actually change?
- What can it never change?
- How does it relate to soul identity, reincarnation, and the Isekai premise?
- Why is it secret, and who benefits from keeping it secret?
- Does the Supreme Trinity relate to it in a way ordinary beings cannot comprehend?

These questions are deliberately preserved. A final operating system can define boundaries without spoiling the central mystery.

======================================================================
9. HOW THE PILLARS INTERACT
======================================================================

A. AUTHORITY + ENERGY
Authority permits a valid action; Energy supplies capacity. Either may be missing. Permission without capacity can fail to execute; capacity without permission can be blocked or cause consequences.

B. TAPASYA + ENERGY
Tapasya may improve control, endurance, stability, or efficiency. It does not automatically increase every Energy dimension at the same rate.

C. TAPASYA + AUTHORITY
Practice may prepare a character to exercise a responsibility, but practice alone does not grant jurisdiction or office.

D. BOON + ENERGY
A Boon may provide an effect, reduce a particular requirement, or supply a defined capability. It does not automatically provide infinite Energy or skill.

E. BOON + AUTHORITY
A Boon may grant a narrow exception. The exception applies only to its defined scope and does not automatically rewrite all jurisdictional rules.

F. KARMA + BOON
A Boon may change the route or conditions of an outcome. It does not automatically erase unrelated consequences. Specific narrative examples must be decided individually.

G. KARMA + TAPASYA
Disciplined action can alter future choices and consequences, but Tapasya is not a universal karma-cleansing token. Any claimed resolution must be shown through the story's causal logic.

H. KARMA + AUTHORITY
An office-holder may administer a process involving Karma without owning, inventing, or arbitrarily rewriting every causal consequence.

I. ATMA-TATTVA + THE OTHER FIVE
The precise interaction remains an intentional secret. Do not write an ordinary combo chart for the Sixth Pillar. Earlier clues may establish limits, anomalies, and misinterpretations without revealing its full nature.

J. ALL SIX TOGETHER
There is no automatic “six-pillar ultimate attack.” A character may interact with several pillars in one event, but each keeps its own role and limits.

10. ABILITIES, TECHNIQUES, WEAPONS, AND KNOWLEDGE
======================================================================

An ability is an observable effect or learned capability. It is not itself a seventh pillar.

For every major ability, record:
- Name and intended meaning.
- Effect.
- User/access condition.
- Activation method.
- Required knowledge.
- Energy demand, if any.
- Authority requirement, if any.
- Tapasya/preparation requirement, if any.
- Boon/exception, if any.
- Karma-related consequence, if any.
- Range, target, duration, precision, and counterplay.
- Failure modes and aftermath.
- Source inspiration vs fictional invention.
- Status.

Weapons/Astras:
Use epic weapon narratives as separate case studies for acquisition, knowledge, invocation, control, target restrictions, withdrawal, counteraction, and consequences. Do not assume every weapon follows the same rules or that knowing its name is sufficient.

Mantra and ritual:
Do not design a universal spellbook and claim it represents all Hindu traditions. In Universe B, a fictional mantra-based technique must specify the tradition-inspired design, learning conditions, performance requirements, target, limitations, and failure conditions. It is a fictional mechanic unless a precise source claim is separately verified.

Siddhi / extraordinary perception:
Keep perception, inference, memory, prior-birth knowledge, and unusual cognition distinct. A character seeing something unusual does not automatically know its full meaning or future consequences.

11. POWER ASSESSMENT AND RANKING
======================================================================

[DESIGN LOCK — RECOMMENDED]
Do not use one universal rank that declares who is strongest in every context.

For character profiles, assess these separate axes:
1. Authority scope.
2. Energy reserve.
3. Energy throughput.
4. Energy control/stability.
5. Tapasya development by domain.
6. Boon effects and conditions.
7. Knowledge and technique mastery.
8. Tactical judgment and experience.
9. Relevant Karma/contextual obligations.
10. Atma-tattva: “unmeasured/secret” unless the story explicitly reveals more.

A public institution may compress these into a practical threat classification, but that classification is an imperfect tool, not cosmic truth. Combat outcome also depends on match-up, environment, preparation, information, objective, and constraints.

Suggested encounter outcome checklist:
- What does each side want?
- What is each side allowed to do?
- What can each side actually sustain?
- What does each side know?
- Which conditions are active?
- What counters are available?
- What does victory cost?
- What consequences remain after the fight?

12. SOUL ADMINISTRATION AND THE POWER SYSTEM
======================================================================

Established Universe B framework:
- Yama is chief administrator of Soul Administration.
- Vidhata handles migration/selection and connects a soul to a new body through a karmic bond.
- Chitragupta personally creates the Soul ID and sends it to Ananta for review.
- Ananta is non-conscious and records actual changes; it follows Supreme Orders.
- Official Migration Eligibility and Cosmic Compatibility are distinct.
- Universe A is not governed by Universe B's custom administrative rules.

Cosmic Compatibility has five author-defined components:
1. conduct/actions and consequences;
2. desire/attachment;
3. three gunas;
4. remaining versus completed karma-results;
5. liberation question (normal rebirth cycle or distinct liberation-associated path).

These five components are not the same as the six Power System pillars. They are not a power score.

Migration rules:
- Eligibility asks whether a soul can enter the process under the applicable rules.
- Compatibility asks how well the soul fits a potential destination/body/circumstance under Universe B's fictional rules.
- Neither question should be silently replaced by “strongest soul gets best body.”
- Atma-bhagya concerns karma-influenced fortune, especially the Kul/family of birth, but its exact relationship to official migration and Isekai arrivals remains open.
- Event-triggered/cosmic reincarnation arrivals may use a separate route. Do not force them into the official process without author approval.
- Cosmic Ocean is a separate transit concept for certain arrivals, not Universe B's internal geography. Any effects during transit require a specific rule; no automatic power-up.

======================================================================
15. COUNTERS, LIMITS, AND FAIRNESS
======================================================================

Every major ability should have at least one meaningful constraint, unless the story deliberately establishes it as an absolute exception.

Useful counter types:
- Jurisdiction counter: the target/action lies outside the user's valid Authority.
- Condition counter: a Boon's condition is not met or its exclusion applies.
- Capacity counter: the user cannot sustain the effect.
- Control counter: the effect can be disrupted or outmanoeuvred.
- Knowledge counter: the user lacks the correct method or interpretation.
- Preparation counter: the opponent has prepared a valid response.
- Context counter: environment, target, timing, or objective changes what works.
- Consequence counter: the action succeeds, but produces a cost or obligation.
- Narrative unknown: a mystery prevents a reliable prediction, but must be foreshadowed rather than used as random rescue.

Avoid:
- arbitrary immunity introduced at the last second;
- a new pillar invented to solve a plot problem;
- equating “more energy” with victory in every matchup;
- making all ancient knowledge automatically superior to all new knowledge;
- making moral goodness a guaranteed combat buff;
- using Karma as authorial punishment;
- making Atma-tattva a convenient answer to every mystery.

======================================================================
16. CANON-CREATION WORKFLOW
======================================================================

A new mechanic becomes canon only after this process:
1. State the proposed rule in one sentence.
2. Mark it [SOURCE CONTEXT], [PROPOSAL], or [OPEN].
3. Identify the pillar(s) it belongs to and why.
4. State access, activation, scope, limits, counters, and consequences.
5. Test it against Universe A / Universe B boundaries and established canon.
6. Test at least two edge cases and one exploit.
7. Check whether it duplicates an existing rule.
8. Approve it explicitly before moving it to [ESTABLISHED CANON].

Research source workflow:
- Record primary text, chapter/verse, recension/edition, translation, and context.
- Distinguish text from later commentary and living tradition.
- If the exact passage has not been verified, mark [PARK — VERIFY SOURCE].
- If a concept is school- or lineage-specific, mark [PARK — TRADITION-SPECIFIC].
- Never convert a fictional mechanic into a claim about Hinduism.

17. TEST CASES — DOES THE SYSTEM HOLD?
======================================================================

TEST A: A character has immense Energy but no relevant Authority.
Expected: the character can perform actions within their capacity but cannot automatically issue valid cosmic commands or override jurisdiction.

TEST B: An office-holder has Authority but low Energy/control.
Expected: the office-holder's valid permission remains real, but they may be unable to execute a demanding personal technique.

TEST C: A character performs Tapasya for years and demands a Boon.
Expected: Tapasya does not force a grantor to award a Boon unless the story has a specific rule establishing that outcome.

TEST D: A Boon protects against a defined weapon.
Expected: the protection does not automatically extend to every weapon, all indirect harm, or every possible consequence.

TEST E: A character wants to “pay off” Karma with Energy.
Expected: no direct exchange exists unless a specific fictional rule is created and approved; default is no.

TEST F: A character has an extraordinary perception ability.
Expected: perception does not automatically equal interpretation, omniscience, or reliable prophecy.

TEST G: Ananta records a new ability.
Expected: Ananta records the actual change; it does not grant the ability or decide whether the user deserves it.

TEST H: An Isekai soul arrives through an event-triggered route.
Expected: do not automatically assume official Migration selection, a Cosmic Ocean power-up, or a fully written destiny. Use only the route rules that the story has established.

TEST I: A character claims Atma-tattva to win.
Expected: the claim itself proves nothing. The pillar remains secret and cannot be used as an unearned universal override.

TEST J: A powerful Asura acts compassionately, or a Deva acts selfishly.
Expected: morality is individual and contextual; species/office does not dictate moral character or combat rank.
