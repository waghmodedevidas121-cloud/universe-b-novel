============================================================

1. “Āgama” आणि “Tantra” या संज्ञा अनेक ग्रंथपरंपरांशी संबंधित आहेत; त्यांचा वापर सर्व संप्रदायांत एकसारखा नाही.
2. शैव, शाक्त आणि वैष्णव आगमिक परंपरांना एका universal ritual manual मध्ये मिसळू नये.
3. एखाद्या विधीचा अर्थ सांगताना विशिष्ट ग्रंथ, अध्याय/पटल, परंपरा, भाषांतर आणि भाष्य नोंदवावे.
4. दीक्षा, मंत्र, न्यास, यंत्र, ध्यान आणि पूजा यांचे धार्मिक/आध्यात्मिक अर्थ source-specific असतात.
5. काही परंपरांत विशिष्ट मंत्रासाठी दीक्षा किंवा गुरु-परंपरेतील मार्गदर्शन महत्त्वाचे असते; ही अट प्रत्येक परंपरेला तंतोतंत लागू होते असे म्हणू नये.
6. गुप्त/दीक्षित पद्धतींबद्दलचे सर्व तपशील सार्वजनिक किंवा एकसमान नसतात. अनिश्चित तपशील बनवून भरू नयेत.

ENTRY 01 — ĀGAMA / आगम: WORSHIP, RITUAL AND TRADITION
============================================================

General source frame:
Āgama literature includes multiple traditions, notably Śaiva, Śākta and Vaiṣṇava lineages. Texts can address worship, temple practice, ritual procedure, iconography, initiation, mantra and theological ideas. Exact coverage differs by text.

Source-supported observations:
- आगम-साहित्य एकाच ग्रंथाचा किंवा एका संप्रदायाचा पर्याय नाही.
- अनेक आगमिक ग्रंथ पूजा, मंदिर-विधी, प्रतिमा/मूर्ती-संबंधित नियम, दीक्षा किंवा मंत्रपद्धती यांवर चर्चा करतात.
- प्रत्येक आगमात वरील सर्व विषय असतीलच असे नाही.
- आगमिक पद्धती प्रत्यक्ष धार्मिक परंपरांमध्ये जिवंत असू शकतात; त्या केवळ fantasy spellbook म्हणून समजणे अयोग्य आहे.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- सर्व हिंदू पूजा एकाच आगमावर आधारित आहेत.
- प्रत्येक आगमिक विधी combat power देतो.
- आगम आणि वेद यांचे नाते प्रत्येक परंपरेत एकसारखे आहे.
- एखाद्या modern summary वरून संपूर्ण आगमिक परंपरेचा निष्कर्ष काढता येतो.

[OPEN] Universe B मधील fictional ritual systems कोणत्या प्रमाणात source-inspired असतील हे अजून ठरलेले नाही.

ENTRY 02 — TANTRA AS A DIVERSE FAMILY OF TRADITIONS
============================================================

Academic anchors:
- David B. Gray, “Tantra and the Tantric Traditions of Hinduism and Buddhism,” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Religion.
- Hugh B. Urban, “Tantra in South Asia,” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Asian History.
- Glen A. Hayes, “Tantra,” Oxford Bibliographies in Hinduism.

Source-supported observations:
- Tantra is a diverse and contested category, not one uniform practice.
- Some traditions emphasize liberation, ritual, meditation, mantra and theological practice; others include different ritual and social approaches.
- Popular culture often reduces Tantra either to “black magic” or to sexual technique. Both reductions are misleading.
- Hindu Tantric traditions should not be automatically conflated with Buddhist Vajrayana, nor should one Hindu lineage be treated as representative of all others.
- Historical dating and origins are scholarly questions; unsupported claims of a single ancient origin should be avoided.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- Tantra means “dark magic.”
- Tantra means only sexuality.
- Every Tantric tradition has identical rituals or beliefs.
- Every Tantra contains a practical combat technique.
- A novel’s fictional “Tantra rank” is a traditional Hindu category.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] When drawing inspiration, identify the particular tradition or concept rather than using “Tantra” as a generic label for any mysterious magic.
[OPEN] Whether the novel uses actual tradition names or original fictional terminology remains undecided.

ENTRY 03 — MANTRA / मंत्र
============================================================

Concept frame:
A mantra can be a sacred utterance, syllable, phrase or text used in a particular religious practice. Its function depends on the tradition, source, ritual setting and interpretation. The word “mantra” should not be treated as a universal synonym for a spell.

Source-supported observations:
- Mantras appear in diverse Vedic, Āgamic, Tantric and devotional settings, but the practices and theories are not identical.
- In some Tantric traditions, mantra is connected to initiation, visualization, deity practice and ritual participation.
- In some lineages, the mantra is transmitted through a teacher and is not treated as an unrestricted public formula.
- The sound, meaning, ritual role and theological interpretation may all matter, depending on the source.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- Every mantra produces a physical blast.
- Any person can use every mantra in any context.
- All mantras share one energy mechanism.
- A fictional incantation is a scriptural mantra.
- Modern “frequency” claims are established merely because a tradition treats sound as sacred.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] A fictional mantra entry may record: source inspiration, language/sound, intended purpose, who can teach it, practice context, limits and story effect. These fields are novel design tools, not a traditional universal schema.
[OPEN] Universe B's mantra acquisition and activation rules remain undecided.

ENTRY 04 — DĪKṢĀ / दीक्षा: INITIATION
============================================================

Concept frame:
Dīkṣā is used in several religious traditions for initiation, consecration or formal entry into a practice/lineage. Its exact meaning, ritual and expected result vary by tradition.

Source-supported observations:
- In some Tantric lineages, initiation enables a practitioner to participate in particular ritual acts or receive a mantra within that lineage.
- Initiation may involve a teacher, ritual steps and a new religious identity or responsibility.
- Different lineages can prescribe different procedures; a single contemporary example cannot stand for every Tantric tradition.
- Some historical texts discuss initiation in ways that connect it to liberation, purification, ritual eligibility or transformation; the details depend on the text.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- Dīkṣā is a universal licence to use all supernatural powers.
- Every initiation causes instant awakening or supernatural strength.
- Every Hindu practice requires Tantric initiation.
- A fictional character can receive a real-world religious initiation just by completing a fantasy quest.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] If fictional initiation exists, distinguish access/eligibility, knowledge transmission, ritual responsibility and any fictional effect. Do not assume these are the same thing.
[OPEN] Universe B has no approved universal dīkṣā mechanic.

ENTRY 05 — NYĀSA / न्यास
============================================================

Concept frame:
Nyāsa is a ritual practice found in particular Hindu ritual and Tantric contexts, often involving placing or assigning mantras, deities or sacred correspondences to parts of the body. The exact procedure and theological meaning vary.

Source-supported observations:
- Nyāsa can be part of preparing the practitioner for worship or ritual participation in specific traditions.
- It may involve the body being ritually understood in relation to a deity, mantra or sacred structure.
- It should be described using a specific source or lineage, not as one universal procedure.
- Ritual embodiment is not equivalent to a scientific claim that the body gains measurable supernatural energy.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- Nyāsa is an armour spell.
- Touching body points always grants powers.
- Every nyāsa uses the same mantras or body map.
- A fictional body-marking system is identical to a traditional rite.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] A fictional ritual may use symbolic mapping of a character's body, but must be labelled as adaptation and not as a direct scriptural power.
[OPEN] No Nyāsa-derived combat system is canonized.

ENTRY 06 — YANTRA / यंत्र
============================================================

Concept frame:
A yantra is commonly described as a geometric or diagrammatic ritual object/design in specific Hindu traditions. Its form, use and meaning vary by tradition and purpose.

Source-supported observations:
- Yantras may be used in worship, meditation or ritual contexts.
- The design is not merely decorative in traditions that assign it a sacred function.
- Different yantras are associated with different deities, ritual systems and interpretations.
- A modern graphic that resembles a yantra is not automatically a traditional yantra.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- A yantra is a literal battery or power generator.
- Every yantra opens a portal or traps a supernatural entity.
- All geometric sacred designs are interchangeable.
- A fictional magic circle is a faithful representation of a specific living tradition.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] If the story includes ritual diagrams, use original fictional designs unless a particular real tradition and its iconography have been carefully researched.
[CAUTION] Avoid using sacred diagrams as generic decoration or attributing unsupported abilities to them.
[OPEN] No yantra-based ability is canonized.

ENTRY 07 — ŚAKTI / शक्ति
============================================================

Concept frame:
Śakti can refer to power, capacity or divine potency; in Śākta traditions, the Goddess and divine feminine power occupy central theological roles. The meaning varies across philosophical and devotional contexts.

Source-supported observations:
- Śākta Tantra includes diverse goddess-centred traditions.
- Śakti may be understood in theological, cosmological, ritual or devotional ways; these are not all the same claim.
- The goddess traditions and their ritual settings differ regionally and historically.
- The term cannot be reduced to a generic energy meter without losing its religious context.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- Śakti is always a measurable substance.
- Every female character is inherently more powerful or spiritually pure.
- Every deity's power works through one universal “Shakti energy” mechanic.
- All traditions agree on one relation between Śiva and Śakti.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] If the novel uses “Shakti” as a named concept, define whether it is a theological reference or a fictional system term. Avoid claiming that a novel-specific mechanic is the one universal Hindu meaning.
[OPEN] The relationship between Universe B's Energy pillar and the concept of Śakti is not fixed.

ENTRY 08 — MUDRĀ / मुद्रा
============================================================

Concept frame:
Mudrā can refer to gestures or symbolic bodily configurations in different ritual, yogic, dance and devotional contexts. Meaning and practice depend on context.

Source-supported observations:
- Mudrās occur across multiple Indian religious and performance traditions, not just Tantra.
- A gesture's ritual meaning cannot be inferred from its shape alone.
- The same term can be used in different contexts with different technical meanings.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- Every hand gesture is a spell.
- Every mudrā gives a combat buff.
- All mudrās have identical effects across Yoga, Tantra, dance and iconography.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] Fictional hand-signs can be created for the novel, but should be named as fictional rather than presented as authentic ritual mudrās.
[OPEN] No mudrā-based combat ability is canonized.

ENTRY 09 — ŚRĪVIDYĀ AS A CASE STUDY IN TRADITIONAL SPECIFICITY
============================================================

Academic source:
Studies of Śrīvidyā describe a particular Śākta Tantric tradition centred on Tripurasundarī and distinctive ritual, mantra and yantra practices. It is one example, not a stand-in for all Tantra.

Source-supported observations:
- Śrīvidyā has its own textual, ritual and lineage histories.
- Initiation practices and mantra transmission may differ between lineages.
- Contemporary practice and historical texts should not be assumed identical in every detail.
- The tradition includes theological and ritual meanings that should not be reduced to a fantasy magic system.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- All Śākta traditions are Śrīvidyā.
- Every practitioner follows the same procedure.
- The tradition can be summarized accurately by one yantra or one mantra.
- A fictional “goddess system” is equivalent to Śrīvidyā.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] Use Śrīvidyā as an example of why a fictional tradition needs a coherent history and context, not as a template to copy without deeper study.
[OPEN] No direct Śrīvidyā-based fictional system is approved.

ENTRY 10 — RITUAL EFFICACY, LIBERATION AND WORLDLY RESULTS
============================================================

Concept frame:
Some Tantric traditions include practices associated with liberation, devotional realization, ritual participation or worldly aims. These aims and methods vary across texts and lineages.

Source-supported observations:
- “Tantra” cannot be reduced to worldly magic; liberation and religious transformation are important in many traditions.
- Some texts and lineages discuss practices for specific worldly goals; these should be described in their own context.
- A ritual's theological meaning, community role and intended result may differ from a fantasy story's visible effect.

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- All Tantric practices are for combat.
- All practices promise the same outcome.
- Historical religious claims are experimentally verified supernatural effects.
- A fictional result is proof of a real-world claim.

Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] Any fictional ritual should specify its in-world purpose and consequences, while keeping source claims separate.
[OPEN] No universal ritual-efficacy rule is approved.

CROSS-ENTRY FINDINGS
============================================================

1. Āgama and Tantra are not single, uniform systems.
2. Mantra, dīkṣā, nyāsa, yantra, mudrā and Śakti are distinct terms; they should not be collapsed into “magic.”
3. Some lineages emphasize initiation and restricted transmission, but no one rule should be projected onto all traditions.
4. Sacred ritual and iconography should not be used as generic fantasy decoration without understanding the context.
5. A source's religious meaning and a novel's fictional mechanic must be documented separately.
6. Universe B's Energy pillar is not automatically identical to prāṇa or Śakti.
7. Universe B's Secret Sixth Pillar, Atma-tattva, remains separate from these categories unless the user explicitly decides otherwise.
8. This file proposes no new canon ability, rank, spell, initiation rule or ritual mechanic.
