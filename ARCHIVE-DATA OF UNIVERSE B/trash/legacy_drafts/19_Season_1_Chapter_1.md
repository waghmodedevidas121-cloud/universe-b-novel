### [SEASON 1 • VOLUME 1: THE MUD, THE BEAST & THE D-TIER BLOOD-PIT]
#### CHAPTER 1: THE HAY, THE HORNS, AND THE DEAD GLASS

Another blast of scalding air slammed into his face.

Neel tried to force his eyes open, but his skull felt as though someone had split it open with a paving brick.

And whatever was standing directly in front of him... it definitely wasn't human.

2 massive, pitch-black horns. Rough like volcanic basalt, pulsing with a dull, subterranean amber glow deep within the stone grain.

And right between those horns—boiling steam vented violently from two massive nostrils.

*FUSSSSSSH!*

Goosebumps erupted across Neel's arms. His breath locked tight in his throat. He didn't dare twitch a muscle.

"Oh... shit," he warned himself in his head. "Don't move, Neel. Don't move an inch."

His right cheek was pressed flat against wet, rotting straw. His soaked denim clung freezing to his legs, his jaw rattling from the chill. The creature towering over him was the size of a small hillock. If it took a single misplaced step, he'd be reduced to paste in less than 0.5 seconds.

Neel wasn't on the ground.

He was sprawled across the backboards of a colossal, rough-hewn freight wagon, buried in dirty straw. The mud beneath the wheels was so deep the entire rig sat half-sunken.

From every direction came the sucking groans of wagons trapped in mire. In the distance, the sharp crack of a bullwhip echoed across the wind, accompanied by coarse voices bellowing filthy curses.

Neel cautiously flexed his fingers. Everything seemed intact. Just battered all over, like he'd been trampled in the morning rush-hour stampede at Dadar station.

He tilted his gaze upward.

No ceiling fan. No concrete station bridge. No Dadar high-rises.

Above hung an unbroken expanse of cold, slate-grey sky. No sun, no clouds, not a sliver of blue. Just a sterile, dead illumination leaking down through his soaked clothes, sending tremors through his core.

"Where the hell did I get dumped?" he mumbled beneath his breath, barely parting his lips. "Does Dadar's drainage canal open straight into hell?"

Without making a sound, he slid his hand into the inner pocket of his sodden denim jacket.

His fingers brushed against a bent plastic card. His quarterly Mumbai local train pass—Dadar to Matunga. Wet, cold... and utterly worthless here.

Right next to it sat his battered Redmi smartphone.

Neel pulled it out. Spiderweb fractures splintered across the glass, mud caked along the edges. He jammed his thumb into the power button.

1 second... 3 seconds... 5 seconds.

The screen remained dead black. Zero battery.

"Brilliant," Neel whispered bitterly to the dead glass. "Folded the moment I actually needed you. 11 rupees and a dead phone... even across worlds, my luck refuses an upgrade."

He stared into the empty air. Somewhere in the back of his mind, a faint, absurd hope had lingered—maybe a translucent blue window would pop up. Maybe an RPG status screen would unfold. Maybe a synthesized voice would announce *'Welcome, Player.'*

Nothing happened. No glowing interface. No magical chime. Not a damn thing.

The wind remained freezing, bitter, and indifferent.

"Good. So there's no game running either," he exhaled, slipping the brick back into his pocket. "At least my sanity's still in one piece."

Neel pushed himself up on one elbow. He edged his chin over the rough wooden rim of the wagon bed—

And the panorama stole the breath right out of his lungs.

For miles in every direction stretched knee-deep, churning black mud. Hundreds of colossal freight wagons, massive horned draft beasts, and mountain-high cargo piles were snarled in a hopeless jam. Drovers screamed at the top of their lungs, hammering heavy timber wedges beneath sunken iron rims.

And roughly 0.5 miles ahead across the churned muck, towering above the mist... rose the monolithic white stone ramparts of Central City.

So immense that the crenellations vanished into the overcast ceiling. And set dead-center within that colossal wall stood a 100-foot-tall gate of black basalt, yawning open as the desperate multitude crawled toward its threshold.

Neel's eyes widened in sheer disbelief.

"Bro... what district is this?" he muttered to the grey air. "This sure as hell isn't Thane."

Right then—

From the slurry directly beneath the wagon's rear axle came a low, ragged snarl.

*Grrrrrr...*

Neel tilted his head over the tailgate to look beneath the wagon.

Anchored to the axle hub by a rusted iron ring was a fraying, rotten length of hemp cord. And on the other end of that tether... a tiny black pup sat huddled in the mire.

One ear torn at the tip, its ribcage protruding sharply through wet fur, shivering uncontrollably from the biting chill. Yet its amber eyes... they were locked dead onto Neel.

It bared its gums, letting out a faint, rasping growl. There was no real strength in the sound—only the hollow agony of an empty belly.

A sharp cramp knotted Neel's own stomach.

Ever since standing in the downpour outside Dadar station with 11 rupees squeezed in his fist, his gut had been completely hollow. His mouth was parched, his intestines burning with hunger. The look in that shivering beast's eyes was a mirror of his own reality. Both of them were strays, neither had a soul to lean on, and neither had a crumb to eat.

*CRACK!*

The vicious whip-snap tore through the air from the front of the cart.

"Pull, you bastard! Heave!"

Neel edged further along the straw to look forward.

The wagon's rear left wheel was buried hub-deep in a sucking clay trench. Up front in the traces, the lone Lava-Bull's massive hooves skidded helplessly across the slick mud. The brute was the size of a small boulder, ember-veins glowing dull within its basalt horns as it vented boiling steam, but the 2-ton timber wagon didn't budge an inch.

Standing on the wagon runner was a short, barrel-chested drover—clad in a grease-stained leather apron, whip in hand. He was lashing the beast's flanks with senseless fury.

*CRACK!*

A plume of scalding sulfur erupted from the bull's nostrils. It wrenched its neck in pain, but the heavy wooden cart remained fused to the trench.

"Scum!" The drover spat into the muck and booted the wagon frame in disgust. "Paid 2 Panas for that cursed runt of a hound, and the wheel sinks straight to the axle! Won't die, won't pull!"

Beneath the wagon, the tethered pup flattened its ears at the boot-thud, but never pulled its glare from the drover. Behind the hunger in those amber eyes burned an unyielding, feral spite.

*CRACK!*

The whip struck wood again, and the wagon master's exasperated roar ripped across the flats:

"Is there a single pair of hands out there, or do I drop dead in this mud alone?!"

Neel took a slow, deliberate breath. Hunger made his head spin, but he knew with cold certainty that if he stayed huddled in the straw like a dead weight, this carter would pitch him into the ditch like refuse.

His eyes darted to the axle wedged into the clay, then settled on a stout, 4-inch-thick timber lever lying discarded in the muck nearby.

Back in the Dadar garage, whenever a heavy cab dropped a wheel into an open storm drain, the mechanic never wasted time looking for a hydraulic jack. He always reached for one thing—a long, solid lever.

Neel dropped down from the tailgate straight into the mire.

*SPLASH!*

Cold, viscous mud surged past his calves to his knees. Freezing slurry flooded his torn sneakers, sending a violent shudder up his spine. He ground his molars together and fought for balance.

"Hey, you! Drifter!" The carter's glare locked onto him. "What the hell do you think you're doing? Back away from my freight!"

Neel ignored him completely. His focus was locked onto a 4-inch-thick, weathered oak timber half-submerged in the slush 3 paces away.

He seized the pole with both hands and wrenched it free of the mud's suction. The timber was heavy, slick, and cold, but the grain was rock-solid.

Back in the Dadar garage, whenever a cab dropped a front wheel into an open gutter, the old mechanic would bark—*'Listen, dumbass, you don't beat dead weight with muscle; you beat it with angles!'*

Neel scanned the trench. Half-buried right behind the stuck rim was a flat, heavy slab of ballast stone. Neel kicked the stone into the clay against the iron rim, levering the tapered end of the oak spar directly beneath the axle hub against the stone pivot.

A textbook class-one lever.

The wagon master stormed back along the cart, whip raised. "Are you deaf? That's a 2-ton freight wagon! A scrawny runt with a stick isn't going to budge—"

"Stop shouting and get to the traces!" Neel snapped back between ragged breaths, his eyes glued to the hub. "When I count to three, haul the beast straight forward. Don't use the whip, just keep the reins taut!"

The teamster stopped short. The cold fury in the 19-year-old's eyes wasn't that of a beggar. Cursing under his breath, the man wheeled around and charged back toward the yoke.

Neel wrapped both hands tight around the long end of the timber spar.

His boots were losing purchase in the grease. To steady his stance, his left hand instinctively braced against the lower sideboard—right beside the iron ring where the pup was tethered.

The hound flinched, and Neel's bare palm brushed squarely against its wet, scarred forehead.

*ZZZZZT!*

No blinding flash. No thunderous burst.

Yet for that 0.2-second interval, across all 72,000 nadis running from his tailbone to the crown of his skull, a piercing, thread-thin tremor of live current zipped through his flesh.

Right behind his temples resonated a razor-sharp acoustic chime—*The Sing*.

For half a breath, Neel felt the wild flutter of the pup's heart synchronize seamlessly with his own pulse. The starving beast's ferocious, desperate will to survive poured directly into Neel's sinews. The numbness in his arms vanished, replaced by an electric surge of focused adrenaline.

The pup's amber eyes went wide, but its snarl fell completely silent.

"NOW! HEAVE!" Neel screamed with everything in his chest.

The carter hauled back on the yoke reins with all his bulk. "Pull, Shera! Pull!"

The Lava-Bull dug its basalt hooves deep into the stone bed, its spinal cord knotting like braided steel cable.

In that single 1-second window, Neel hurled all 58 kilos of his body weight down across the end of the spar.

*CRACK-GROAN!*

The heavy oak groaned under the agonizing 2-ton strain. Veins corded across Neel's neck, his sneakers sinking 6 inches deeper into the freezing sludge, but he refused to yield an inch.

*SHLUUURP!*

The mud's vacuum seal ruptured.

Air sucked beneath the iron tire, and with one monumental, shuddering heave from the horned beast, the 2-ton wheel surged up out of the pit and slammed onto the crushed gravel rim!

*THUD!*

The wagon rolled forward 3 full feet, coming to rest safely on solid stone.

Neel collapsed to his knees in the mud. The skin on both his palms was scraped raw, his lungs heaving like bellows, his limbs trembling violently from exhaustion.

But the wagon was free.
