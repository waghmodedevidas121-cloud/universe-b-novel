A. PURPOSE AND STATUS
======================================================================

This master document brings together the seven catalogue parts created for
the novel's scriptural research layer. It preserves the detailed entries
from the source parts and adds a common framework, terminology guide,
cross-part index, and canon-protection rules.

This is a structured research draft, not a final scholarly concordance and
not a declaration that every item has been verified against critical editions.
The seven source parts are selective. Verse numbering, translations, textual
recensions, and interpretations may vary. Publication-grade use requires
checking the exact passage and edition.

IMPORTANT: integration does not mean that every scriptural idea becomes a
Universe B ability. The source catalogues intentionally distinguish textual
material from possible fictional adaptation. No new ability, character,
faction, office, rank, ritual rule, soul-migration rule, or cosmic event is
approved as canon by this document.

======================================================================
B. DOCUMENT STATUS LABELS — USE CONSISTENTLY
======================================================================

[CANON]
A rule explicitly established by the author for the novel. It is canon
because the author established it, not because a scriptural source contains
a similar-sounding term.

[SOURCE-SUPPORTED]
A limited statement supported by a named textual passage or a clearly
identified tradition. It does not automatically establish a modern
supernatural claim or a fictional mechanic.

[TRADITION-SPECIFIC]
A teaching or interpretation associated with a particular school, lineage,
commentator, historical period, or community. Do not generalize it to all
Hindu traditions.

[PROPOSAL]
A possible fictional adaptation. It remains optional until the author
approves it.

[OPEN]
A question or design decision that has not been settled.

[CAUTION]
A common conflation, unsupported inference, or sensitive interpretive issue
that should not be repeated as fact.

Use these labels at entry level wherever practical. If a source part uses
slightly different wording, interpret it in this common framework without
silently changing its claim.

C. SEVEN-PART SCOPE AND MASTER INDEX
======================================================================

PART 1 — SCRIPTURAL ABILITY CATALOGUE
Focus: selected scriptural descriptions of perception, knowledge, vision,
weapons, memory, and spiritual or narrative outcomes.
Core distinction: seeing, knowing, remembering, acquiring knowledge, and
using an ability are not automatically the same capability.

PART 2 — MAHABHARATA ABILITIES
Focus: selected epic cases including Pashupata Astra, Brahmashira, Narayanastra,
Karna's curse and memory failure, Bhishma's iccha-mrityu, Gandiva, celestial
weapons, and Savitri's argument and boons.
Core distinction: weapon knowledge, invocation, direction, withdrawal,
neutralization, and understanding consequences can be separate.

PART 3 — PURANIC POWERS AND BOONS
Focus: selected boon narratives and divine episodes, including Hiranyakashipu,
Narasimha, Dhruva, Markandeya, Dadhichi, Vrtrasura, Devi Mahatmya/Mahishasura,
and the general narrative category of curses.
Core distinction: a boon can have a specific condition or narrative outcome;
it is not automatically a reusable combat skill or universal rule.

PART 4 — VEDAS AND UPANISHADS
Focus: Shruti structure, Atman, Brahman, Tat Tvam Asi, Prana, mind and senses,
Neti Neti, Om/upasana, moksha/rebirth, knowledge, teacher, mantra, and ritual.
Core distinction: philosophical or spiritual concepts are not automatically
combat energy, mana, immortality, or a game-like ability.

PART 5 — AGAMAS AND TANTRAS
Focus: Agama and Tantra traditions, mantra, diksha, nyasa, yantra, Shakti,
mudra, Sri Vidya as a specific case, and ritual efficacy.
Core distinction: these are diverse traditions, not one universal “magic
system”; context and lineage matter.

PART 6 — YOGA AND DARSHANAS
Focus: Patanjali Yoga, Samkhya, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva Mimamsa, Vedanta
schools, pramana, klesha/abhyasa/vairagya, siddhi/vibhuti, and kaivalya.
Core distinction: philosophical schools differ; terms such as guna, siddhi,
kaivalya, and moksha are not universal ranks or combat categories.

PART 7 — VEDIC DEITIES AND MANTRAS
Focus: selected references for Agni, Indra, Varuna, Savitr/Gayatri, Ushas,
Soma, Rudra, Vishnu, Sarasvati, Surya, and contextual notes on Yajurveda,
Samaveda, Atharvaveda, and mantra.
Core distinction: a deity's Vedic portrayal is not automatically identical
to later Puranic developments or a fictional Universe B role.

D. INTEGRATED RESEARCH PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

D1. SOURCE LAYERS MUST NOT BE COLLAPSED
Record these as separate layers:
1. Primary passage or root text.
2. Translation and edition.
3. Commentary or school-specific interpretation.
4. Later epic/Puranic/Agamic/Tantric development.
5. Modern scholarly interpretation or practitioner explanation.
6. Fictional adaptation for the novel.

A claim can be well supported at one layer and still not establish a claim
at another. For example, a text may describe a divine weapon in a story;
that does not establish a universal weapon taxonomy for all traditions.

D2. SCRIPTURAL CONCEPTS ARE NOT AUTOMATIC GAME MECHANICS
Do not automatically translate:
- Atman into mana, stamina, or a soul battery.
- Brahman into Brahma, the creator deity.
- Prana into an anime energy beam.
- The three gunas into three game elements.
- Siddhi into a guaranteed modern superpower.
- Mantra into a universal command spell.
- Tapasya into a simple experience-point system.
- Boon into a freely repeatable skill.
- Divine weapon into a generic projectile.
- Moksha, kaivalya, or liberation into a combat rank.
- Cosmic order into an invented administrative rule without author approval.

D3. SIMILAR TERMS MAY HAVE DIFFERENT MEANINGS
The same transliterated word may be used differently across periods,
schools, genres, or ritual traditions. Conversely, related ideas may use
different words. Always attach a source and context to a technical claim.

D4. NARRATIVE OUTCOME IS NOT ALWAYS A GENERAL RULE
A one-time boon, curse, divine intervention, weapon consequence, or unusual
birth may be specific to its story. Do not infer a universal rule from a
single narrative unless the source or author separately supports it.

D5. PRESERVE THE SACRED CONTEXT
Do not flatten sacred texts, mantras, deities, or ritual traditions into
generic fantasy decoration. Where exact ritual instructions are restricted,
uncertain, or beyond the research scope, do not invent missing details.
Fictional parallels must be labelled as fictional.

E. CROSS-PART CONCEPT MAP
======================================================================

PERCEPTION AND KNOWLEDGE
- Part 1: divine sight, forms of knowing, mind-related knowledge, memory,
  and specific knowledge described in selected texts.
- Part 2: knowledge of divine weapons versus the ability to invoke, direct,
  withdraw, or neutralize them.
- Part 4: knowledge, mind, senses, Atman/Brahman, and liberation.
- Part 6: pramana, cognition, Yoga, and siddhi descriptions.
Integration rule: keep perception, cognition, memory, spiritual insight,
and practical skill as separate categories unless a source explicitly
connects them.

WEAPONS, BOONS, CURSES, AND CONDITIONS
- Part 1: selected weapon-related and knowledge-related descriptions.
- Part 2: epic weapon cases, boons, curses, and consequences.
- Part 3: Puranic boons, curses, protection conditions, and divine outcomes.
Integration rule: document acquisition, eligibility, activation, control,
withdrawal, cost, limitation, and aftermath separately. These are research
fields, not a claim that every source uses this taxonomy.

RITUAL, MANTRA, AND PRACTICE
- Part 3: ritual/devotional context in selected Puranic narratives.
- Part 4: Vedic and Upanishadic mantra, upasana, and ritual context.
- Part 5: Agama/Tantra, diksha, nyasa, yantra, mudra, and ritual practice.
- Part 6: disciplined practice and philosophical context.
- Part 7: selected Vedic hymns and mantra context.
Integration rule: “mantra” and “ritual” must not become one uniform spell
system. Name the specific tradition and source.

SOUL, SELF, LIBERATION, AND REBIRTH
- Part 1: selected passages involving death-time orientation or future
  destination and knowledge related to prior birth, where documented.
- Part 4: Atman, Brahman, moksha, rebirth, and related philosophical themes.
- Part 6: Yoga, Samkhya, Vedanta, kaivalya, and liberation.
Integration rule: distinguish philosophical claims from fictional
Soul Administration. Do not infer migration eligibility or cosmic
compatibility from a philosophical passage without an approved rule.

DIVINE FIGURES AND COSMIC ORDER
- Part 2 and Part 3: epic and Puranic divine narratives.
- Part 4 and Part 7: Vedic and Upanishadic references and later-development
  cautions.
Integration rule: never assume that the same deity name implies identical
rank, role, attributes, or theology in every text or in Universe B.

F. CONNECTION TO THE NOVEL'S SIX-PILLAR WORKING POWER SYSTEM
======================================================================

The novel's current working framework contains five initial pillars:
1. Authority
2. Energy
3. Karma
4. Tapasya
5. Boons
plus the secret sixth pillar, Atma-tattva.

The following is a research crosswalk only. It does NOT declare that the
scriptural catalogues validate or define the fictional pillars.

AUTHORITY
Possible research conversations: divine status, ritual office, authority
to use a weapon or perform a rite, and role-specific permissions in a story.
Caution: a divine name or a single mythic event does not establish a
universal hierarchy for Universe B.

ENERGY
Possible research conversations: fire, solar imagery, prana, Shakti, ritual
substances, and divine weapon descriptions.
Caution: these terms are not interchangeable and do not automatically
equal the fictional Energy pillar.

KARMA
Possible research conversations: action and consequence, ethical narrative,
rebirth, and philosophical discussions in relevant traditions.
Caution: do not merge every account of fate, divine judgment, ritual result,
or rebirth into one universal karma formula.

TAPASYA
Possible research conversations: disciplined practice, austerity, vows,
devotion, and narrative cases where practice precedes an encounter or boon.
Caution: the sources do not establish one universal numeric progression,
power level, or guaranteed reward schedule.

BOONS
Possible research conversations: boon narratives, conditions, limits,
unintended outcomes, and the difference between a boon and a skill.
Caution: a boon in one story does not create a universal template for all
boons in all traditions or in Universe B.

ATMA-TATTVA — SECRET SIXTH PILLAR
The author has designated Atma-tattva as the novel's secret sixth pillar.
It is a fictional narrative classification inspired by philosophical inquiry,
not a claim that Hindu scripture officially calls it “the sixth Power System
pillar.” Atman must not be reduced to a mana pool, combat energy, or ordinary
ability. The exact fictional mechanics remain secret/open unless the author
defines them.

G. UNIVERSE A / UNIVERSE B FIREWALL — NON-NEGOTIABLE
======================================================================

UNIVERSE A
- Mainstream/traditional Hindu mythology universe.
- Its deities, sacred timeline, cosmic arrangements, and Soul Administration
  remain untouched by the user's custom Soul Migration System.
- Do not apply Universe B's invented Ananta or migration mechanics to
  Universe A.

UNIVERSE B
- An independent fictional universe inspired by Hindu mythology and
  shastric traditions; it is not a claim that all sources form one canon.
- Birth Side and Death Side are separated by Kshira Sagara.
- Karana Ocean is not part of Universe B's internal geography.
- Cosmic Ocean is a separate transit concept for certain event-triggered
  soul arrivals, not an internal region of Universe B.
- Direct Rama, Krishna, and Kalki avatars do not enter Universe B under
  the author's Zero-Avatar Protocol; the reason remains open.
- Supreme Trinity: Brahma — Supreme Creator; Vishnu — Supreme Preserver;
  Shiva/Mahesh — Supreme Destroyer. They are Almighty and outside ordinary
  Power System limits; this is Universe B canon, not a conclusion inferred
  from any one catalogue entry.
- Shiva/Mahesh is associated with Mahasamhara and does not routinely appear
  at every event. The Supreme Trinity does not need Tapasya to become
  Almighty and can show compassion or help.
- Ananta is a non-conscious System, not a soul, person, or deity. It has
  no independent will or emotions, follows Supreme Orders, records actual
  reality changes, and cannot independently alter core rules, grant powers,
  determine destiny, or cause arrivals.
- Soul Administration: Yama is chief administrator; Vidhata handles
  migration/selection and connects a soul to a new body through a karmic
  bond; Chitragupta personally creates Soul ID and sends it to Ananta for
  review. Tenure/retirement/succession details remain open.
- Official Migration: Vidhata coordinates migration based on Karma and
  Cosmic Compatibility. Migration Eligibility is a separate gate and is
  not the same as Compatibility.
- Cosmic Compatibility's five components: conduct/actions and consequences;
  desire/attachment; three gunas; remaining versus completed karma-results;
  liberation question (normal rebirth cycle or a distinct liberation-
  associated path). Do not turn this into the Migration Eligibility list.
- Route B: event-triggered/cosmic reincarnation arrivals may not be selected
  through official migration. Some may pass through the separate Cosmic
  Ocean transit; transit may alter, grant, or not affect abilities/powers/
  authority. A strong influence does not automatically mean greater combat
  strength. Exact triggers/mechanics remain open.
- Atma-bhagya means karma-influenced fortune, especially the Kul/family a
  soul is born into; it does not mean the future is already written.
- Isekai souls have a blank Ananta page and are not bound by a prewritten
  destiny; their relationship with Route B remains open.
- Devas are a celestial bureaucracy/federation; Indra's role is under
  revision. Asuras are not automatically evil. Proposed Asura factions are
  not approved canon. Rishis' soul purity, social authority, combat rank,
  and jurisdiction are separate dimensions.
- The Power System's six-part working framework remains as listed in
  Section F. The source catalogues add no seventh pillar.

I. FINAL VERIFICATION WORKFLOW
======================================================================

Before promoting a research entry to a stronger status:
1. Identify the exact source and passage.
2. Read the passage in context, not only a search snippet.
3. Record edition, translation, and recension where relevant.
4. Separate root text, commentary, later tradition, and modern summary.
5. Record variant interpretations when materially relevant.
6. Check whether a claimed power is actually stated or merely inferred.
7. Mark the fictional adaptation [PROPOSAL].
8. Check Universe A / Universe B separation.
9. Check that existing migration and Power System rules have not been
   altered accidentally.
10. Obtain author approval before changing canon.

ENTRY 09 — CURSE AND NARRATIVE CONSEQUENCE: A GENERAL PURANIC CATEGORY
============================================================

Primary-source approach:
Curse narratives appear in multiple Puranas. A reliable catalogue must list each curse as an individual episode, not merge all of them into one rule.

Source-supported observation:
पुराणिक कथांमध्ये शाप अनेकदा विशिष्ट वक्ता, कारण, परिस्थिती आणि फलिताशी जोडलेला असतो. तो कथानकात नैतिक, धार्मिक, सामाजिक किंवा cosmic परिणाम घडवू शकतो; पण सर्व शाप एकाच प्रकारे कार्य करतात असे म्हणता येत नाही.

Required fields for every curse entry:
- Who pronounces it?
- What event or conduct prompts it?
- What exact consequence is stated?
- Is there a condition, duration, mitigation, or later transformation in the source?
- Does the story show the consequence directly or only describe it?
- Which text and edition are being used?
- Is the proposed Universe B mechanic new?

What this DOES NOT automatically establish:
- Every curse is reversible.
- Every curse can be lifted by another boon.
- Every curse causes a direct combat debuff.
- A curse always reflects a simple good-versus-evil judgment.

Possible Universe B use:
[PROPOSAL] Curses should be catalogued by source and episode, with no universal mechanics assumed.
[OPEN] Universe B's curse rules remain undecided; no curse-system canon is approved in this part.

ENTRY 03 — MANTRA / मंत्र

Concept frame:
A mantra can be a sacred utterance, syllable, phrase or text used in a particular religious practice. Its function depends on the tradition, source, ritual setting and interpretation. The word “mantra” should not be treated as a universal synonym for a spell.

Source-supported observations:

- Any person can use every mantra in any context.
- All mantras share one energy mechanism.

ENTRY 03 — NYĀYA / न्याय
